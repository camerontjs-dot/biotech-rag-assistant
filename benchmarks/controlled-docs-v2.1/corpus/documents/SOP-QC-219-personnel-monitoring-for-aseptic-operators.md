# Personnel Monitoring for Aseptic Operators

## Purpose and scope

Operators are the largest single source of contamination in an aseptic suite, and their gloves and gowns are the surfaces that come closest to open product. This procedure sets out how operators working in ZN-A1 and in the Grade B rooms RM-301, RM-304 and RM-291 are sampled, how the results are judged, and what happens to an operator whose result is at or above an action level. Room, air and surface monitoring is covered by SOP-QC-214, and the qualification of operators to gown and work in the suite is covered by SOP-TR-021.

## When sampling takes place

| Activity | Sampling required |
| --- | --- |
| Leaving ZN-A1 after any intervention | Glove print of both hands |
| End of every filling session | Glove print of both hands and gown samples at three sites |
| Operator working only in a Grade B room | Glove print at the end of the session, gown samples once a week |
| Return to ZN-A1 after an action-level print | Glove prints under the return rule below |

An operator who leaves ZN-A1 several times in a session gives a glove print at each exit. Prints are taken before the operator sanitises or changes gloves, because the purpose is to measure what the gloves carried, not what they carried after cleaning.

## Materials

Finger and glove prints use 55 mm TSA contact plates (MAT-2233). Gown samples use the same plate type. Plates are drawn from the batch prepared for the session, are labelled with the operator's identifier and the sampling point, and travel to the incubator in the closed box described in SOP-QC-214. Sterile 70 percent isopropyl alcohol is available at the gowning bench for wiping gloves after sampling.

## Glove print technique

The analyst or a qualified colleague holds the plate; the operator does not hold their own plate.

1. The operator presents the right hand, fingers spread, over the plate.
2. The pad of each of the five fingertips is pressed onto the agar in turn, with even pressure, for 5 seconds each.
3. The operator repeats the sequence for the left hand on a second plate.
4. Both plates are closed at once and labelled with hand, time and location.
5. The operator wipes the gloves with sterile 70 percent isopropyl alcohol and lets them air dry for 30 seconds before touching anything else.

A print that misses a fingertip, slides on the agar or is taken after the gloves have been wiped is not valid, and the operator gives a further print on a fresh plate. The invalid plate is discarded and noted on the log.

## Gown sampling

Three sites are sampled at the end of a session: the forearm of the dominant arm, the centre of the chest and the hood at the level of the forehead. The plate is pressed flat for 5 seconds and lifted without sliding. Sampling never happens over open product, and the operator leaves ZN-A1 before any gown sample is taken. Gown samples are taken in RM-291, at the bench where the operator gowned, before the operator removes the gown.

## Limits

The limits below apply to prints and gown samples read at the end of the incubation period. Incubation conditions are those in SPC-2231.

| Site | Alert level | Action level |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gloves used in ZN-A1 | not applicable | 1 CFU per glove |
| Gloves in Grade B rooms | 1 CFU per glove | 2 CFU per glove |
| Gown sites (forearm, chest, hood) | 4 CFU per plate | 9 CFU per plate |

A glove result is the sum of the counts on the plates for that hand. Where the two hands differ, the higher hand decides the level of the result.

## Response to an action-level result

An operator whose glove print or gown sample reaches its action level is informed by the QC supervisor as soon as the plate is read. For a print from ZN-A1 the operator leaves ZN-A1 for the remainder of the session, if the print is read while the session is running, and does not return to any Grade A duty until the return rule has been met. The result is recorded on FRM-QC-126 and the response on FRM-QC-121 and, where product was open when the print was taken, reported to the QA reviewer, who decides whether a deviation is needed under SOP-QA-101. The organism is identified on the same basis as any other Grade A or Grade B isolate.

## Return to the zone

An operator returns to ZN-A1 only after 3 consecutive compliant glove prints, taken over separate sessions, none of which reaches the alert level or the action level for its site. The prints are taken at the gowning bench after routine gowning, and they are separate from the prints required on leaving ZN-A1. During the return period the operator may work in Grade B rooms. An operator who fails a return print starts the count again from zero.

## Recording

Every print and gown sample is entered on FRM-QC-126 within 15 minutes of sampling, with the operator's identifier, the hand or site, the plate lot, the time and the sampler's initials. Results are added to the same log after reading, and the QC supervisor countersigns the log for each session. A missing entry is treated as a missing sample: the operator's next session begins with a print at the gowning bench so that the gap is closed.

## Trending

Results are trended for each operator and each site every month. A rising count for one operator across several sessions is discussed with that operator's supervisor even when no single result reached the alert level, because the sign of poor technique is usually a drift in counts. Group patterns, for example an increase for the whole second shift, are sent to the QC microbiology manager for the monthly report described in SOP-QC-214.

## Operators in training

Trainees who are still in the qualification programme of SOP-TR-021 are sampled at each qualification session under that procedure and their results go on FRM-TR-101, and their results are not entered in the routine trend. A trainee who is also working supervised in a Grade B room gives a print at the end of each session like any other operator. The interval at which an operator with repeated action-level prints is referred for formal behaviour review follows the aseptic behaviour guideline ABG-0011.
