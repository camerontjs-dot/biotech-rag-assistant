# Calibration Technician Quick Reference

## Use of this card

This card is for technicians new to the calibration round. It fits on one clipboard page and is meant to be read at the instrument. The full rules are in SOP-EN-131 and the balance and pH meter test lists are in CAL-0507; if this card and those documents ever differ, work to the documents and tell your supervisor.

## The round in one minute

Start from the weekly due list, collect the check-weight set EQ-0820 and the buffers MAT-2601, MAT-2602 and MAT-2603, and go to the instruments in room order so you are not walking back through a Grade D corridor with clean weights. Record the as-found reading before you touch anything, then adjust, then record the as-left reading. Both go on FRM-EN-201 for every instrument, including the ones that pass on the first look.

## How often

- Analytical balances and top-loading balances: every 9 months.
- pH meters: every 5 months.
- Differential pressure gauges and pipettes: every 12 months.
- Reference thermometer and laboratory timers: every 12 months.
- Cold room and chamber probes: every 12 months.

You may calibrate an instrument up to 7 days before its due date and keep the original schedule. After the due date there is no grace: the instrument is red-labelled and out of service until the work is done.

## Labels

Green means in calibration. Yellow means limited use, and you write the restriction on the label in ink. Red means out of service. Fill in the date done, the due date and your initials, and never peel a label off one instrument to reuse it on another.

## pH meters

Use the three buffers in order: 4.01, 7.00, 10.01. Rinse the electrode with purified water between each. The slope has to fall between 93 and 105 percent for the meter to pass. Check the pH 7.00 buffer read as a sample; it must agree within 0.05 pH. Buffers go in the bin 30 days after opening, and buffer decanted into a beaker goes in the bin after 8 hours.

## Balances

Give the balance 60 minutes to warm up after power-up. The daily check at first use is 100 g and 1 g: the 100 g reading has to be within 0.3 mg and the 1 g reading within 0.05 mg. If the daily check fails, tag the balance and call the supervisor. Do not weigh anything on a tagged balance, however small the job looks.

## When something reads out

If the as-found reading is outside tolerance, tell your supervisor before you leave the room. The impact assessment on FRM-EN-205 has to be started within 3 working days of your finding, and it is finished within 20 working days. The assessment looks back at every use since the last good calibration, so make a note of anything you know was weighed or measured on the instrument recently, such as whether it was used for a release sample.

## Do not

- Move a label from one instrument to another.
- Use a red-labelled instrument, even for a quick check.
- Adjust the manufacturer's internal weight during a routine calibration.
- Leave an electrode to dry out.
- Sign FRM-EN-201 before the label on the instrument is updated.
