# Receiving Dock Briefing

## Who should read this

Anyone who works the receiving dock or the materials airlock RM-330 and books deliveries into stock. It is a short briefing on what to do when a truck arrives, and it points to the procedure behind each step. The controlling documents are SOP-WH-071 for receipt, sampling and release, and SOP-WH-064 for the approval of suppliers.

## When a delivery arrives

Check the delivery in this order.

1. Is the supplier on the approved supplier list? If it is not, do not accept the delivery and call the warehouse supervisor.
2. Does the paperwork match the purchase order: material, quantity, lot number?
3. Are the containers intact, clean, sealed and labelled?
4. Is there a certificate of analysis for the lot?
5. For a cold-chain material, is there a logger, and does it show a trace within the allowance?

The receipt inspection is recorded on FRM-WH-510, and it must be done within 1 working day of the delivery arriving. If you cannot get to it in that time, tell your supervisor before the day ends.

## How long it takes

A typical receipt inspection takes about 20 minutes per pallet. If a logger is missing or a seal is broken, tell your supervisor within 30 minutes so that the carrier can still be contacted before the driver leaves.

## Quarantine straight away

Every lot goes into stock as quarantined. Put a yellow quarantine label on each container, showing the material code, the lot and the date of receipt, and move the pallet to the marked quarantine area. Never leave a delivery on the dock overnight without a label, and never mix it with released stock, even for a few hours.

## What sampling looks like

You will not usually take samples yourself; the samplers do that in the booth RM-334. What you can do is help: leave the lot accessible, keep a count of the containers, and tell the sampler if a container looks different from its neighbours. For lots with 11 to 30 containers, 6 are sampled, and for lots with 31 to 100 containers, 11 are sampled. Vials (MAT-4030) and stoppers (MAT-4055) are checked by inspection of a sample from each lot. Excipient containers such as MAT-4012 are all identity tested, so do not be surprised when QC asks for every container to be made available.

## Cold-chain deliveries

Read the logger before you sign for the delivery. If the trace shows an excursion beyond the allowance, do not accept it on the dock. Put it in quarantine, write down what you saw and call the supervisor, since QA will decide. A delivery with no logger at all is treated as failing.

## When QC releases a lot

Only the person who applies the release label removes the quarantine label. Both actions happen together, and the system must show the lot as released before anyone picks from it. QC releases a lot within 10 working days of sampling. If a lot is still in quarantine after that, ask QC where it stands, since it may mean a test is waiting.

## Retest dates

Excipient containers carry a retest date. When you pick, check it. A container that is past its retest date goes back to quarantine and is not used until QC has retested it.

## Things not to do

- Do not accept a delivery from an unapproved supplier.
- Do not remove a quarantine label yourself.
- Do not sign for a cold-chain delivery before reading the logger.
- Do not open a container to look inside; report the concern and let the sampler do it.
- Do not put a lot into stock with a pen mark instead of a label.
