# Complaint Intake Note

## For customer service and medical information staff

You are often the first person to hear about a problem with a product. What you write down in the first 10 minutes decides how quickly and how well the complaint can be investigated. This note lists what to collect and what to say. The procedure it follows is SOP-QA-146, and returned goods are covered by SOP-QA-151.

## What to collect

Aim to complete the intake within 10 minutes of the call. If you cannot reach the reporter, try 3 times over 2 days and note each attempt on the record. If a call drops, ring back within 15 minutes.

Ask for the following, and write down exactly what you are told.

1. Who is reporting, and how can we reach them again?
2. What is the product name, and what does the carton or label say for the lot number and expiry?
3. What is wrong, in the reporter's own words?
4. When and where was the product used or found, and by whom?
5. Was anyone harmed or unwell? If so, note what was said and stop asking for detail; call the QA contact at once.
6. Is the product still available, and where is it stored now?

If the reporter cannot give a lot number, ask for a photograph of the carton or the label. Do not guess a lot from a purchase date.

## What to say

You may say that the complaint has been logged, that you will give the reference, and that someone will contact them within 2 working days. You may not say what caused the problem, that the product is safe or unsafe, that it will be replaced or that compensation will follow. If the reporter asks a medical question, pass it to the medical information team.

## Logging

Check the record again 1 day later in case the reporter has replied. Enter the complaint on FRM-QA-601 on the day you receive it, and never leave it until the next morning. The record will receive a number of the form CM-nnnn; give it to the reporter. If you hear of a second complaint on the same lot, link it to the first record instead of opening a new one.

## When you must escalate at once

A complaint that mentions harm to a patient, a suspected mix-up of products, foreign material in an injectable, or any sign that the product may have been tampered with is treated as Critical. Tell the QA contact immediately, by phone. The QA Head is informed within 4 hours of classification, so the first call should not wait for the form to be finished.

## Asking for the sample

If the reporter still has the product, ask for it to be sent back. The complaint sample is requested within 3 working days, and the reporter is asked to return it within 14 days. Give clear instructions: keep the product in its carton, store it as the label says, and do not throw away the container or any packaging. Tell temperature-controlled products' reporters to keep them refrigerated until collected.

## Timelines to keep in mind

A missed call is returned within 30 minutes during working hours, and a voicemail left over a weekend is logged first thing on the next working day.

- Same working day: log the complaint.
- Within 1 working day: classification by the customer quality officer.
- Within 2 working days: acknowledgement to the reporter.
- Within 5 working days of closure: written response to the reporter.

## Things that go wrong

- Recording the reporter's opinion of the cause as a fact.
- Forgetting to ask for the lot and expiry.
- Promising a replacement before the investigation is complete.
- Leaving a phone message unlogged over a weekend.
- Opening a new record for a complaint that is already in the system.
