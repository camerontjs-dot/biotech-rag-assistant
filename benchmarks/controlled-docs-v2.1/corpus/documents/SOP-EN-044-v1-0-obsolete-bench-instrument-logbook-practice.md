# Bench Instrument Logbook Practice

## Purpose

Instruments that sit on a laboratory bench are used by many people over a day, and the only lasting record of that use used to be the logbook beside the instrument. This practice describes how the bound logbooks are issued, filled in, reviewed and closed. It applies to balances, pH meters, hot plates, stirrers and similar bench equipment in the Quality Control laboratories and in Formulation.

## Issue of logbooks

Logbooks are bound books of 100 numbered pages, issued by the Engineering document clerk against a request from the instrument owner. The clerk writes the instrument number on the cover, records the date of issue and the first and last page numbers in the register, and hands the book to the owner. A logbook is never photocopied, and a book with a torn or missing page is returned and reported at once.

## Entries

Every use of the instrument is entered in black ink at the time of use. Each entry carries the date, the time, the initials of the user, the purpose of the use and any fault noticed. The entry for a balance also records the reading of the daily check weight. A user who finds an earlier entry missing writes a dated note beside it and does not fill the gap from memory. A mistake is corrected with a single line, the correct entry, initials and date; correction fluid and overwriting are not allowed.

## Calibration entries

The calibration technician writes each calibration into the logbook on the day it is done, stating the as-found and as-left readings, the standards used and the next due date. A copy of the certificate is pasted into the book beside the entry. The technician initials the entry and applies the colour label to the instrument.

## Review

The instrument owner reads the logbook every month and signs the bottom of the last page used. A section head reviews all the logbooks in a laboratory once every three months, looking for gaps in the sequence, use of a red-labelled instrument, entries without initials and long stretches without any entry that suggest the book is being filled in afterwards. Findings are written on the review sheet and returned to the owner.

## Closing a logbook

When the last page is used the owner writes a closing line stating the last page number and the date, signs it and returns the book to the Engineering document clerk. The clerk issues a new logbook with the next serial number, and the closed book is stored in the Engineering archive room for 2 years after the last entry before it is destroyed.

## Instruments without a logbook

A hot plate or stirrer with no measuring function may be covered by a single shared logbook for the room, provided the entry for each use names the instrument. Instruments of this kind are checked visually at the start of each week.

## Lost or damaged logbooks

A logbook that is lost, or that is damaged so that entries cannot be read, is reported to the instrument owner and to the Engineering document clerk on the same day. The clerk issues a replacement book with a note on the cover referring to the lost book. The owner writes a statement of what was known about the missing entries, and the instrument is checked against the daily check weight or a reference before use. A book found later is returned to the clerk, who links it to the replacement.
