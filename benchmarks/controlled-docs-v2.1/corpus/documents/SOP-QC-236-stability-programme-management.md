# Stability Programme Management

## Purpose

This procedure describes how stability studies are designed, set up, run, evaluated and closed, so that the shelf life of each product is supported by data that were generated under controlled conditions. It covers the choice of batches, the labelling and loading of samples, scheduling and executing pulls, chamber monitoring and excursions, the handling of out-of-trend and out-of-specification results, and the reports that summarise each study. The storage conditions, pull points and pull windows are set in SPC-2271, and the assessment of chamber excursions is supported by TCN-4110.

## Scope

The procedure applies to the drug product stability studies of the site, run in the chambers CH-01 to CH-05, the photostability cabinet PC-1 and the stability store RM-415 (Grade D). It applies to the commercial products PRD-311, PRD-427 and PRD-604 and to any new product from the day its first stability batch is made. Stability studies of starting materials and of reference standards are managed under their own arrangements and are outside this procedure.

## Definitions

Time zero: the date on which samples are placed in a chamber, from which every pull point is counted.

Pull: the removal of a set of samples from a chamber at a scheduled time point.

Pull window: the interval around the nominal pull date within which a pull is valid.

Out-of-trend (OOT): a result that is within specification but departs from the pattern of earlier results for the same batch or product.

Excursion: any period in which a chamber's temperature or humidity is outside its tolerance.

Reserve sample: a unit set aside at each pull, kept in the chamber until the results are accepted, for use if a test has to be repeated.

## Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Stability coordinator | Owns the schedule and the inventory, issues pull requests, compiles reports |
| QC analyst | Tests pulled samples, records results, flags OOT and OOS results |

## Study set-up

### Study design and protocols

Every study is described in a written protocol that states the product, the batches, the containers, the storage conditions, the pull points, the tests and their methods, and the acceptance criteria. Protocols are prepared by the stability coordinator, approved by the QC chemistry manager and QA, and issued as controlled documents under SOP-QA-105. The protocol for PRD-311 is STP-0311. A protocol may be amended, but only by a formal amendment that is approved by the same people and that states whether the studies already running are affected.

For each attribute the protocol gives the test method, the acceptance criterion and the number of units consumed at each time point, so that the number of units placed in a chamber at time zero covers every pull with the reserve. It also states the presentation being tested, meaning the vial size, the fill volume and the closure, and the orientation in which the vials are stored. Vials are stored upright for the whole study unless the protocol asks for an inverted position for the first 12 months, in which case half of the units are stored each way.

### Batch selection

One production batch of each product is placed on stability each year, in line with SPC-2271. The stability coordinator picks it from the batches made in the first half of the year, using a rule fixed in the protocol, such as the first batch of the year made at full commercial scale. Where a change under SOP-QA-118 could affect stability, the first three batches made after the change are also placed on stability, and the results are compared with those of the batches made before it.

### Presentations and bracketing

Where a product is offered in more than one vial size, the smallest and the largest sizes are placed on stability, and an intermediate size is added only when its fill volume differs by more than 25 percent from the nearest size already on study. A change of stopper or of vial supplier is treated as a change of presentation, and the first batch filled with the new component is placed on stability at all conditions of the protocol. The stability coordinator keeps a table of presentations for each product, so that it is always clear which sizes and components are covered by which study.

### Sample labelling and identification

Each unit carries a label that shows the product code, the lot, the study condition, the pull point and a unique unit number, printed with the barcode used by the stability inventory system. Labels are applied before the units go into the chamber and are checked by a second person. Labels must survive 36 months at the storage condition, and a sample of labels from each print run is tested by being kept in CH-03 for 4 weeks before the run is accepted. A unit without a legible label is never returned to the chamber.

### Chamber assignment and capacity

The stability coordinator assigns each study to a chamber according to its condition and keeps the assignment list under change control; the intermediate chamber CH-02 is used only for studies that have triggered the intermediate condition. A chamber is never filled to more than 80 percent of its shelf area, so that air can circulate, and shelves are left with 5 cm of clearance from the walls. Studies with a common condition are grouped by product so that opening the chamber for one product does not disturb others more than necessary. A backup chamber of each type is kept empty for use if a chamber must be taken out of service.

### Cold chambers CH-04 and CH-05

The refrigerated chamber CH-04 and the frozen chamber CH-05 need extra care. CH-04 runs an automatic defrost cycle every 12 hours that lasts 20 minutes, during which the temperature may rise by up to 2 degrees C, and it must recover within 15 minutes of the end of the cycle. The monitoring system flags any defrost peak above 8 degrees C. Units removed from CH-05 stay in their cartons and are held at room temperature for 30 minutes before testing unless the method says otherwise, so that condensation does not form on the vial. Frost on a unit is wiped off with a clean cloth and never scraped, and a unit with a cracked label is reported before it is tested.

### Loading the chambers

The chamber technician loads samples on the day of time zero, after the stability coordinator has confirmed the labels and the loading list. The technician records the chamber, the shelf, the time and the number of units for every lot on FRM-QC-301 and initials each entry. Loading is completed within 60 minutes of opening the chamber door, and the chamber temperature is confirmed within its tolerance before the technician leaves. Samples are placed in the exact orientation stated in the protocol, for example vials upright or inverted.

### Humidity control and the chamber water supply

Humidity in CH-01 to CH-03 comes from a steam humidifier fed from a reservoir of purified water. The reservoir is inspected each week for turbidity and floating matter, drained and refilled every 4 weeks, and descaled every 8 weeks by Engineering. A chamber whose reservoir runs low raises an alarm before the humidity drops out of tolerance, and the technician tops it up from the purified water point noted in SPC-2252 for the laboratory bench. The date of each refill is written on the chamber log, and a reservoir that shows any growth is drained, cleaned and refilled before the chamber is used again.

## Pulls and testing

### Pull calendar and scheduling

From the loading list, the stability coordinator generates a pull calendar for the whole study, and it is reviewed every month. The calendar shows the nominal date and the window for each pull. A pull that would fall on a weekend or a holiday is scheduled early so that it stays within its window, and the technician and the analyst who will do the work are named 10 working days ahead. The calendar is the only source of pull dates, and no pull is carried out from memory or from a private list.

### Pull request and execution

The stability coordinator issues a pull request on FRM-QC-301 5 working days before the nominal date. On the day, the chamber technician removes exactly the units named on the request, checks their labels against the request, records the time of removal and hands the units to the analyst. Any discrepancy between the request and the units in the chamber is reported at once and the pull does not proceed until it is understood. The chamber is closed immediately after the pull, and the reserve units for that time point are left in place.

### Movement of samples between shelves

Chambers have 6 shelves, numbered 1 to 6 from the top. Every unit is assigned a shelf at loading, and any later movement between shelves or trays is recorded on the chamber log with the date, the time and the initials of the technician. Samples are moved only when a shelf has to be cleaned, a chamber is emptied for mapping or a unit has to be quarantined. The inventory system is updated on the same day, so that the recorded location always matches the real one.

### Transport of pulled samples

Pulled samples reach the laboratory within 4 hours of leaving the chamber. Samples from a refrigerated or frozen chamber travel in an insulated box with a temperature indicator, and those from CH-01 to CH-03 travel in a closed tray, out of direct light and heat. Samples are never left on a bench or a trolley while the technician does other work. The analyst records the time of receipt, and a delay beyond the limit is treated as a handling failure and reviewed by the stability coordinator.

### Sample receipt and testing schedule

Testing starts within 10 working days of the pull. Samples that cannot be tested within that time are returned to their condition under a documented arrangement approved by the stability coordinator, and the time out of the chamber is recorded. The analyst tests the samples with the methods in the protocol, uses reference standards that are within their retest interval, and records results on the laboratory record and in the stability database.

### Method changes during a study

A change to an analytical method while a study is running needs a bridging comparison before the new method is used for any time point. The comparison tests at least 3 retained samples by both methods and shows that the results agree within the limits set for the method. The results of the old and the new method are reported side by side for 2 consecutive time points, so that a step in the trend caused by the method is not mistaken for a change in the product. The QC chemistry manager approves the change, and QA reviews it under SOP-QA-118 if the method is part of a filed specification.

### Test panels

The tests done at each time point are those named in the protocol for the product and the time point, and the panel may differ from one pull to the next. A test dropped from a panel needs a protocol amendment, and a test added later is added to every remaining time point. The analyst checks the panel before testing and does not test extra attributes on the pulled units unless the protocol says so.

### Reference standards and reagents

Working standards used for stability testing are qualified against the primary standard, and each carries an expiry date on its label. A standard is not used for a time point if it will expire before the testing is complete, and standards within 30 days of expiry are replaced at the next scheduled preparation. Reagents and mobile phases are prepared on the day of use where the method calls for it, and the preparation record shows the lot of each component. The retest interval for the reference standards themselves follows the reference standard monograph RSM-0012.

## Special studies

### Photostability studies

Photostability studies are run in the cabinet PC-1 on a single batch of each product and each new container. The light exposure, the measurement of the dose and the dark controls are defined by the photostability study PHS-0008. Samples are placed at the position mapped for uniform exposure, and a dark control wrapped in aluminium foil sits beside each exposed sample. The dose readings are recorded at the start and the end of each run, and a run that does not achieve its target dose is repeated. Each run uses 3 samples of each container, and the dose meter is calibrated every 12 months.

### Freeze-thaw studies

A freeze-thaw study exposes samples to 3 cycles, each consisting of 24 hours at minus 20 degrees C followed by thawing to room temperature for 12 hours. Samples are tested after the third cycle and compared with untreated controls kept at the storage condition. The study is run once for each product and again when a change in formulation or container could alter its behaviour. A sample that shows a visible change after any cycle is photographed and reported to the QC chemistry manager before testing proceeds.

### In-use stability

An in-use study tests the product after the container has been opened or punctured. Vials are punctured once with a sterile needle, held at the condition in the protocol for 28 days, and tested at the time points in the protocol. The study reports the number of punctures, the storage between punctures and the appearance of the contents at each time point. The result is used to set the in-use period on the label, and a shorter period is used if any time point fails.

## Chambers and their control

### Chamber monitoring and alarms

Every chamber is monitored by the system EQ-1410, which records temperature and humidity every 5 minutes and raises an alarm when a reading crosses the alarm limit. Alarms go to the on-call technician and to the stability coordinator. The technician acknowledges an alarm, checks the chamber and the reading on the chamber's own display, and records what was found on the chamber log. The monitoring system is reviewed each working day, and the audit trail of alarm acknowledgements is reviewed once a month.

### Chamber mapping and requalification

Each chamber is mapped every 12 months and after any relocation, refit or major repair, following the method in TCN-4110. The mapping report shows where the chamber is warmest, coldest, driest and most humid, and the stability coordinator uses it to decide where the monitoring probe stays and where samples may be placed. A chamber that fails mapping is taken out of service, its samples move to the backup chamber, and it returns to use only after a successful repeat mapping and QA approval.

### Differences between the display and the monitoring system

The temperature shown on a chamber's own display should agree with the reading of the monitoring system EQ-1410. If the two differ by more than 1 degree C, the technician compares both with a calibrated reference thermometer placed at the probe position and records the three readings on the chamber log. The chamber is not used for new studies until the difference is explained, and the Engineering technician checks the probe and the display. Samples already inside remain in place while the difference is resolved, and the stability coordinator decides whether an excursion assessment is needed.

### Door access and handling

A chamber door is not held open for more than 5 minutes for any single operation. Longer operations, such as loading a new study, are planned so that the door is closed and the chamber has recovered before a second operation begins. The technician wears cold-protective gloves for CH-04 and CH-05, and never leaves the door of a chamber ajar while retrieving items from another chamber. After a door has been open, the chamber is checked on the display before the technician moves away from it.

### Power failure and backup

All chambers are on the site's backup power circuit. If mains power is lost, the backup supply restores the chambers within 10 seconds, and the monitoring system records the interruption. A technician checks each chamber within 30 minutes of any power failure that lasted more than 10 seconds. Where a chamber has not recovered, samples are moved to the backup chamber of the same type, and the move is documented on the chamber log.

### Chamber excursions

An excursion is assessed when the temperature of a chamber deviates by more than 2 degrees C from its setpoint for more than 4 hours, or the humidity by more than 5 percent RH for more than 8 hours. The assessment is written on FRM-QC-305 by the stability coordinator and the QC chemistry manager, and reviewed by QA. It compares the duration and size of the excursion with the tiers in TCN-4110 and with the stability behaviour of the product, and it decides whether the affected samples remain valid, are flagged, or are excluded from the shelf life data. Samples are never moved between chambers to conceal an excursion.

### Broken or leaking units

A unit that is broken or leaking, found at loading, at a pull or on a routine check, is removed the same day. The technician inspects the neighbouring units and the shelf for spread, wipes the shelf with 70 percent isopropyl alcohol and records the unit number, the lot and the finding. One broken unit is noted in the study file. Three or more broken units at one time point in one study are reported as a deviation under SOP-QA-101, because the pattern may reflect a container or closure problem and not accidental damage.

## Results and evaluation

### Out-of-trend results

An OOT alert is raised when the assay of a batch falls by more than 2.0 percent between consecutive time points, or when a result breaks the pattern of the batch's earlier results. The analyst reports the result to the stability coordinator on the day it is known. The coordinator checks the calculation, the sample identity and the method performance, and records whether the alert reflects a real change in the product. An OOT result that is confirmed is discussed with the QC chemistry manager and may lead to extra pulls or a change in shelf life.

### Out-of-specification results

A result outside its specification is handled through the OOS procedure OSP-0006, and a deviation is opened under SOP-QA-101. The batch and the studies for the product are reviewed for impact, and QA decides whether product on the market is affected. No sample is retested to get a passing result. The investigation states the cause, the impact on the shelf life and the actions taken, and the result is reported in the stability summary whatever the outcome.

### Trending and evaluation

Results for each attribute are plotted against time for each batch, and the coordinator estimates the shelf life from the data by regression, following the method in the protocol. The extrapolation allowed beyond the longest real-time data for a new product follows the stability protocol STP-0311, and the shelf life is never extended beyond what the data support. The retest interval for a reference standard follows the reference standard monograph RSM-0012. The regression uses the results of at least 3 lots of the product. Trends are reviewed by the QC chemistry manager every quarter.

### Reserve samples and retention

The reserve units from each pull stay in their chamber until the results of that time point have been accepted, and are then destroyed unless the protocol keeps them. Units left in the chamber after the final pull of a study are kept for 12 months and then destroyed under a witnessed record, so that a late question about an early result can still be answered from stored material. The stability coordinator keeps the list of retained material and reviews it every 6 months.

### Inventory reconciliation

Every quarter the stability coordinator reconciles the inventory system with a physical count of each chamber, on FRM-QC-310. The count compares units by lot, condition and time point, and the variance must be zero. A difference is investigated at once, and a missing unit is reported as a deviation under SOP-QA-101 because it may point to a mislabelled or misplaced sample.

### Stability summary reports

A summary report is issued for each study year within 30 working days of the final pull of that year. The report shows the results for every batch and condition, the trend evaluation, any excursions and their assessments, any OOT or OOS results and the conclusion on the shelf life. It is signed by the stability coordinator, the QC chemistry manager and the QA reviewer, and it is available to the batch disposition process as described in SOP-QA-133.

### Stability commitments for new products

In the first year after a product is launched, the first 3 commercial batches are placed on stability as a commitment, in addition to the annual batch, and the annual batch rule of SPC-2271 applies afterwards. The commitment results are reviewed by the QC chemistry manager at the 3-month and the 6-month time points against the data that supported the shelf life. A result that departs from the supporting data is reported to QA within 2 working days of its approval, whether or not it is within specification.

## Programme administration

### Study completion and disposal

A study is complete when its last pull has been tested and the final report has been signed. The samples remaining are kept for the retention period above and are then destroyed. The protocol, the raw data, the reports and the chamber records are archived as set in POL-0004, and the coordinator closes the study in the inventory system.

### Transfer of samples between sites

Samples that must be sent to another laboratory, for example for a special test, are packed in the qualified shipper at 2 to 8 degrees C or at the storage condition of the study, with a logger, following SOP-WH-088. The pack-out, the transit time and the receipt are recorded, and the time away from the chamber is added to the sample history. A shipment that exceeds its excursion allowance is quarantined and the affected samples are assessed before results are used.

### Changes to the programme

A change to a chamber, a monitoring setting, a container, a closure or a protocol is assessed under SOP-QA-118. A change that could affect stability is not implemented until the assessment says what extra studies are needed. Where a chamber is replaced, its mapping and qualification are complete before samples are moved in. Cold storage of stability samples outside chambers, for example in a freezer, follows SOP-WH-092.

### Training and qualification

Stability staff are trained on this procedure, on the operation of the chambers and on the stability database before working unaided. Analysts who test stability samples are qualified on the methods in the protocols. Refresher training is given every 12 months and after any significant revision, and a person who missed a pull window is retrained on the calendar before their next pull.

### Continuity during shutdowns and holidays

The programme does not stop when the site does. Chamber checks continue every day, twice a day at weekends, by the on-call technician, and the alarm system keeps its normal routing. Pulls that fall in a shutdown are carried out by the on-call team according to the calendar, or brought forward within their window. A shutdown longer than 5 working days is announced to the stability coordinator at least 10 working days before it begins, so that the calendar can be adjusted and the analysts scheduled.

### Records

The protocols, pull requests, chamber logs, alarm records, excursion assessments, OOT and OOS records, inventory reconciliations and summary reports are retained for the periods set in POL-0004. The stability database is the working record of results and is backed up every day. Calibration records for the probes and sensors are kept under SOP-EN-131.
