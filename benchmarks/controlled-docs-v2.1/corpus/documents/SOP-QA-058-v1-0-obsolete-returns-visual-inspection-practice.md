# Returns Visual Inspection Practice

## Purpose

Product that has been returned by a customer is looked at before any decision is made about it. This practice describes how returned vials and cartons are visually inspected, what defects are looked for and how the result is recorded. It applies to returned product at the shipping dock and in the returns bay.

## Inspection conditions

The inspection is done at a bench in the returns bay with the light level set at 1,000 lux, measured at the surface of the bench. Behind the vial there are two backgrounds, one black and one white, and the inspector looks against each in turn. The inspector is trained for the task, has had eyesight checked in the last 12 months and works for no more than 2 hours before a break of 15 minutes.

## Method

Each vial is held by the neck and gently swirled, and then inspected for 5 seconds against the black background and 5 seconds against the white. The inspector looks for particles, cracks, chips in the glass, damaged stoppers, missing or loose seals, fill level differences, discolouration and any label damage. Cartons are inspected for crushing, water marks, tampering and torn seals before the vials are removed.

## Recording

The inspector records the number of units inspected, the number with each type of defect and the result on the inspection sheet. A unit with a critical defect, such as a particle or a crack, is set aside and photographed. A lot with any critical defect in the units inspected is rejected. A lot with no critical defect and with cosmetic defects in no more than 2 percent of units is passed to the QA reviewer with the sheet.

## Equipment

The bench carries two backgrounds of 30 cm by 20 cm, one black and one white, fixed at 45 degrees behind the inspection position. The light level is checked at the start of each day with the light meter EQ-1330, and the reading is written on the daily sheet. A lamp that reads below 1,000 lux is replaced before inspection begins, and the bench is wiped with 70 percent isopropyl alcohol before each return is opened. Magnifying glasses are not used, because the practice relies on the unaided eye.

## Sampling

For a return of 30 units or fewer, every unit is inspected. For larger returns, 30 units are taken at random from across the cartons, and if a critical defect is found, the whole return is inspected.

## Limitations

The method relies on the inspector's eye and cannot show the temperature history of the product or the integrity of the seal under pressure. It is used to decide whether a return looks acceptable, and any decision to restock also needs a review of the shipping and storage records.
