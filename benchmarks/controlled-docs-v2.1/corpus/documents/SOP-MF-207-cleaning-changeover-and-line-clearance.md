# Cleaning, Changeover and Line Clearance

## Purpose

This procedure describes how filling line FL-2, the equipment that supports it and the rooms it occupies are cleaned, how the line is changed over from one product to another, and how the line is cleared and released for the next batch. Its aim is that no residue of a previous product, cleaning agent or contaminant reaches the next batch. The agents, concentrations and hold times are set in SPC-2260, and the detailed line clearance check at the start of a batch is in SOP-MF-211.

## Scope and equipment covered

The procedure covers the product-contact parts of FL-2, that is the filling needles and manifold, tubing, pump heads, stopper bowl and chute, and the change parts for vial sizes; the filling barrier EQ-0602 and its glove ports; the parts washer EQ-1310 and the autoclave EQ-1320 in RM-325 (Grade D); and the surfaces of the filling room RM-301 (Grade B), the crimping room RM-318 (Grade C) and the washing and staging room RM-325. Products made on the line are referred to by their codes PRD-311, PRD-427 and PRD-604.

## Equipment inventory and cleaning methods

Each item that is removed from the line has an assigned method, so that operators do not have to decide on the day how a part is to be cleaned.

| Item | Method | Quantity per set | Typical wash time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Filling needles | Parts washer EQ-1310 | 8 needles | 45 minutes |
| Filling manifold | Parts washer EQ-1310 | 1 manifold | 45 minutes |
| Stopper bowl and chute | Parts washer EQ-1310 | 1 bowl, 1 chute | 40 minutes |
| Pump heads | Manual wash in RM-325 | 2 heads | 25 minutes |
| Vial guide rails | Manual wash in RM-325 | 6 rails | 20 minutes |
| Silicone tubing | Replaced with a new sterile set | 1 set per batch | Not washed |

Tubing is discarded at the end of each batch because its inner surface cannot be inspected, and a new set is opened only in the line clearance step of SOP-MF-211.

## Definitions

Dirty hold: the time between the end of processing and the start of cleaning.

Clean hold: the time between the end of cleaning, drying and covering, and the next use of the equipment.

Full clean: the complete sequence of detergent wash, acid step where due, rinses, drying, disinfection and verification, applied to every product-contact part.

Changeover: the transition of the line from one product, or one product presentation, to another.

Campaign: a series of consecutive batches of the same product made without a full clean between them.

Line clearance: the check that the line, the room and the documents are free of materials from the previous batch.

## Principles of the programme

Cleaning is validated, and the validation defines what may be changed by the operator and what may not. The operator does not change concentrations, contact times or sequences on their own judgement; a proposed change goes through SOP-QA-118. Equipment is cleaned as soon as possible after use, since the dirty hold limit in SPC-2260 caps how long residue may dry. Two people share the work when the equipment is heavy or the sequence has many steps, so that no step is missed and no part is dropped. Every step that has a time or a temperature is recorded as it is done, not afterwards.

## Cleaning agents

### Agents and where they are specified

The alkaline detergent MAT-3101, the acid descaler MAT-3105, the sporicidal disinfectant MAT-3110 and 70 percent isopropyl alcohol MAT-3112 are the four agents in use. Their concentrations, contact times and expiry rules are in SPC-2260, and this procedure does not repeat them. An agent that is not on the specification is never used, even when it is on the shelf, and a proposed new agent goes through change control before a first trial. Rinse-only verification of cleaning, described in the earlier memo TCN-4021, has been replaced by the swab method below.

### Storage and control of cleaning agents

Concentrates are stored at 15 to 25 degrees C in a locked cabinet in RM-325. Acids and alkalis are kept on separate shelves at least 1 m apart, and no container is stored above eye level. Each delivery is quarantined until the stores operator has compared the label and certificate with the order within 2 working days, and a lot is released for use only when the certificate matches. Stock is limited to about 3 months of use so that concentrate does not age on the shelf, and the oldest lot is used first. The cabinet is inspected each month for leaks, corrosion and missing labels, and any container that shows damage is moved into a secondary tray at once.

### Compatibility of agents and materials

Not every agent may touch every material. Stainless steel parts can be treated with all four agents. Anodised aluminium parts, which include the vial guide rails, are washed in the alkaline detergent for no more than 20 minutes and never above 60 degrees C, and they never see the acid descaler. Elastomer seals and gaskets are inspected after every acid step for swelling or softening and are replaced if either is found. Silicone components are not left in the acid descaler for more than 15 minutes. Any part that changes colour, becomes rough or loses a coating after cleaning is reported to engineering and set aside, since a damaged surface holds residue.

### Safe use of the sporicidal agent

The sporicidal disinfectant is a strong oxidiser and must be used with the room ventilation on. Operators wear the splash goggles and nitrile gloves listed for RM-325, and never use the agent in a room where the doors are locked. It must not be mixed with the acid descaler or with any chlorine-based product, because the mixture releases a harmful gas. A container that is warm to the touch, swollen or leaking is taken out of use and reported to the safety officer. If the agent contacts skin, the area is rinsed for at least 15 minutes, and an eye splash is rinsed at the eyewash station for the same time before medical advice is sought.

### Preparing solutions and containers

The operator prepares solutions in a clean, dry container in RM-325, weighing or measuring the concentrate exactly as the preparation table in SPC-2260 states. The container is labelled before the concentrate goes in, not after. Solutions for use in Grade B or Grade A areas are passed through a 0.2 um filter into a sterile container before they leave RM-325. Unused solution is discarded at the end of the shift where it was made for use in Grade A or Grade B, and by the expiry date in SPC-2260 in other areas, and containers are washed and dried before their next use.

### Protective equipment and gowning

Operators cleaning in Grade B and Grade A areas are gowned to the standard qualified under SOP-TR-021. In RM-325 they wear a coverall, hair cover, mask, safety glasses and nitrile gloves that reach over the cuffs. Acid descaler and alkaline detergent are corrosive, and the operator uses splash goggles when pouring concentrate and a face shield when a spray is used. An eyewash station stands 6 m from the parts washer and is checked every week, and the operator knows where it is before starting.

## Cleaning methods

### Cleaning product-contact parts

The sequence below applies to all product-contact parts that are removed from the line.

1. Rinse the part with purified water to remove gross residue, within the dirty hold limit.
2. Load the part into the parts washer EQ-1310, or wash by hand in RM-325 where the part cannot go into the washer.
3. Apply the alkaline detergent at the specified concentration and contact.
4. Rinse with WFI the specified number of times and read the final rinse conductivity.
5. Apply the acid descaler in the month it is due, followed by a further rinse.
6. Dry the part, inspect it under the required light and record the result.
7. Wrap and sterilise in the autoclave EQ-1320, or cover and store in RM-325.

A part that is cracked, pitted or scratched in a product-contact area is set aside for engineering assessment, and its serial number is written on FRM-MF-301 so that the history of the part is traceable.

### Timeline of a full clean

A full clean of the product-contact parts follows the steps below. The times are typical, and the schedule allows a block of 8 hours so that a slow step does not force a step to be skipped.

| Step | Typical time |
| --- | --- |
| Rinse with purified water | 15 minutes |
| Alkaline wash | 20 minutes |
| WFI rinses, 3 in total | 30 minutes |
| Acid step and rinse, in the month it is due | 30 minutes |
| Drying | At least 30 minutes |
| Visual inspection | 20 minutes |
| Swabbing | 40 minutes |
| Steam sterilisation of wrapped parts | 3 hours including cool-down |

The production supervisor books the parts washer and the autoclave for the whole block before the last batch of a campaign ends, and starts the dirty hold clock at the end of that batch.

### Layout and segregation of the washing room

RM-325 is divided into a dirty side and a clean side by a pass-through. Parts arrive on the dirty side and leave through the washer or the manual wash bay to the clean side, and nothing moves back. Racks on the clean side stand at least 30 cm above the floor, and each carries the date and time at which its parts finished cleaning so that the clean hold can be read at a glance. The sterile cloth on the racks is changed every shift, and the shelves under the racks are cleaned every week. Personnel do not carry items from the clean side to the dirty side without changing gloves.

### Cleaning the filling barrier

The barrier EQ-0602 is cleaned by hand from the top down. The operator wipes the ceiling panels, the walls, the glove ports and the floor of the enclosure in overlapping strokes, working away from the fill needle line. Each wipe is used once and never returned to the container. The sporicidal disinfectant is applied to the interior surfaces after the detergent and is left wet for the contact time in SPC-2260. Where a surface cannot be reached from outside the barrier, the operator uses the long-handled tool that is kept in RM-301, disinfected before it enters the barrier. Barrier surfaces are never wiped with a dry wipe, since dry wiping raises particles.

### Cleaning of rooms by grade

The frequencies below apply in addition to the cleaning at changeover.

| Area | Floors | Walls | Ceilings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Grade B | After each session | Weekly | Monthly |
| Grade C | Daily | Every 14 days | Every 3 months |
| Grade D | Daily | Monthly | Every 6 months |

A room that has been the location of a spill, a deviation or a shutdown is cleaned outside this table, and the reason is written on FRM-MF-301.

### Control of the room during cleaning

Cleaning is not started until the differential pressure of the room is confirmed to be within its band, with at least 10 Pa between RM-301 and RM-289 on the differential pressure gauge EQ-0831. The doors of RM-301 stay closed while cleaning is in progress, and no more than 4 operators are in the room at any moment. The air handling stays on, and cleaning materials and waste do not cross the airlock at the same time as personnel. An alarm on pressure or airflow during cleaning stops the work until the reading is back in its band, and the interruption is entered on FRM-MF-301 with its start and end times.

### Technique for floors, walls and ceilings

Surfaces are cleaned from the cleanest point to the dirtiest and from the highest point to the lowest. The operator moves in overlapping parallel strokes, never scrubbing back and forth, and folds the wipe to expose a fresh face for each stroke. Floors are mopped last, walking backwards from the far corner toward the door, so that the operator never steps on a surface that has just been treated. The disinfectant is left to dry naturally and is not wiped off, unless the specification says otherwise. Mops are never used for more than one room in a shift, and buckets are emptied and dried between rooms.

### Disinfectant rotation

Grade A and Grade B surfaces receive the sporicidal disinfectant at the frequency in SPC-2260, and the routine disinfectant is rotated with it so that resistant organisms do not become established. The production supervisor keeps the rotation calendar and writes the agent used at each application on FRM-MF-301. Environmental monitoring sampling of a surface follows the disinfection only after the dwell period that SOP-QC-214 requires has passed, and the analyst checks the cleaning record before taking a contact plate, so that a result is never read without knowing what was applied.

### Cleaning in the crimping room

RM-318 (Grade C) is cleaned on its own routine because aluminium debris from crimping is a particle source close to the filling line. The crimping heads are wiped at the end of every session, the cap hopper is emptied and wiped at the end of every batch, and a HEPA-filtered vacuum is used to collect aluminium fragments every 4 hours during crimping. Wet cleaning of the floor follows the vacuuming and never precedes it. A crimping head that has been changed or adjusted is cleaned before use, and the cap lot number is recorded on FRM-MF-301 for each batch.

## Changeover and line clearance

### Changeover types

Two types of changeover are recognised, and the type decides how much cleaning is needed.

| Type | Description | Cleaning required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Type A | Same product, different lot | Cleaning of the product path and surfaces, no swabs |
| Type B | Different product | Full clean of every product-contact part, followed by swabs |

Changing from PRD-311 to PRD-427 or to PRD-604 is always a Type B changeover, and so is a change between two strengths of a product when the formulation differs. The production supervisor states the type on FRM-MF-301 before cleaning begins, and the QA line clearance inspector agrees it. Where the type is uncertain, Type B applies.

### Changeovers away from PRD-604

A changeover away from PRD-604 carries an extra check. The QA line clearance inspector reviews the whole clearance record together with a second inspector, and the line is not released for the next batch until the swab results are available. For every other Type B changeover the line may be released on visual inspection while the swab results are pending, provided no product is filled until the results are in. This rule follows from the residue behaviour of PRD-604 in the cleaning validation, and it is not relaxed for urgent orders.

### Carts, trolleys and stools

Carts, trolleys and stools that move between rooms are a common route for contamination, so each has its own cleaning rule. Items used in Grade B are wiped with 70 percent isopropyl alcohol at the end of every session and with the sporicidal agent once a week. Wheels and castors are cleaned on both sides of the airlock, and an item that has left a room and comes back is treated as newly introduced. Items that live in RM-325 are wiped daily. A cart with damaged paint, rust or a torn cover is removed from use and repaired before it re-enters a classified room.

### Campaign limits and full clean

No more than 6 consecutive batches of one product are made on FL-2 before a full clean, and a full clean is carried out at least every 30 days even if the campaign has not reached its limit or the line has stood idle. The maximum campaign length for PRD-604 is the number in the campaign plan approved by QA for that product, and it may be lower than the general limit. A batch that would take the campaign over its limit does not start until the full clean is complete.

### Handover during cleaning

A cleaning that continues across a change of shift is handed over at a defined stopping point, which is the end of a rinse or the end of drying and never the middle of a wash. The outgoing supervisor records the step reached, the time and any part left in a hold, and the incoming supervisor reads the record within 30 minutes and confirms it on FRM-MF-301. The dirty hold and the clean hold continue running across the handover, and a hold that expires during the handover is counted as exceeded.

## Verification of cleaning

### Swab verification

After a Type B changeover and after every full clean, the QC analyst takes swabs from 3 sites of 25 cm2 each, using the swab kit MAT-3120. The sites are the worst-case locations named in the swab plan: the filling needle seat, the lowest point of the manifold and the underside of the stopper chute. The analyst wears fresh gloves for each site, moves the swab in two perpendicular directions and places it in the extraction tube. The residue limit for the TOC swab is 8 ug per swab. The recovery factor that converts a swab result to the amount on the surface is taken from the cleaning validation report CVR-0019.

### Environmental sampling after a full clean

A full clean of RM-301 is followed by environmental sampling at rest before filling starts, under the shutdown rule in SOP-QC-214. The production supervisor informs the QC supervisor at the start of the clean so that the plates and the analyst are ready, and the analyst confirms on FRM-MF-301 that the disinfectant cycle was completed before the first at-rest sample. Results from the at-rest sessions are entered in the same trend as operating results, with a note that they were taken at rest.

### Visual inspection

Every product-contact part and surface is visually inspected before it is released. The inspection is done at 500 lux or more, measured at the surface with the light meter EQ-1330, from a distance of about 30 cm and at two angles. The part must show no residue, film, discolouration, particulate or water spotting. A part that fails is cleaned again from the alkaline step, and the failure is recorded. The inspector signs FRM-MF-301 only after seeing the part, not after seeing the paperwork.

### Drying and covering

After the final rinse, parts are dried in clean air until no visible water remains and are left to dry for at least 30 minutes before they are covered or wrapped, since trapped moisture supports growth during the clean hold. Parts are laid on lint-free sterile cloth on a stainless steel rack in RM-325, never on the floor or the edge of a sink. Wrapped parts carry a label with the part name, the date and the expiry from the clean hold rules, and the label is visible without unwrapping.

## Equipment, waste and events

### Barrier gloves and glove ports

The gloves fitted to the glove ports of EQ-0602 are changed every 8 hours of production, whatever their appearance, and after any visible damage or suspected breach. The gloves are inspected before each session by stretching and looking for pinholes. The port and the sleeve joint are disinfected when a glove is changed, and the glove serial number is recorded on FRM-MF-301 so that the wear of each pair can be followed. Glove integrity testing follows the schedule in the equipment maintenance plan.

### Cleaning tools and wipes

In Grade A and Grade B, mop heads and wipes are single use and sterile, and they are discarded after one use. In Grades C and D, reusable mop heads are permitted, and each is retired after 30 launderings, counted on the tag that is attached to the mop head. Brushes and sponges are not permitted in any classified room, because they retain residue and organisms. Wipes are supplied in sealed packs that are opened only in the room where they will be used, and the pack is closed between uses.

### Waste handling

Waste from cleaning, including used wipes, wet mops, gloves and expired solutions, is placed in labelled bags inside the room. Bags are sealed and removed from the room within 2 hours of the end of the cleaning activity, so that contaminated material does not sit in a classified area. Liquid waste is drained to the neutralisation tank through the marked drain and is never poured into a sink used for product. Sharps go into the rigid container, which is replaced when it is three-quarters full.

### Spills

A spill of product, detergent or acid is contained at once with the absorbent material in the spill kit, and the area is marked off. The operator informs the production supervisor, and cleans the area according to the safety data sheet for the material spilled. The spill kit is restocked within 1 working day of any use, and the supervisor checks it every month. A spill in ZN-A1 or in the barrier is reported to QA the same shift, since it may affect product, and QA decides whether a deviation is required under SOP-QA-101.

### Broken glass in the barrier

When a vial breaks inside EQ-0602, the line is stopped at once and two operators deal with it. Glass is collected with the tongs and a HEPA-filtered vacuum reserved for this purpose, never with the hands, and the fragments are counted and compared with the number of vials known to have been involved. The area is then wiped 3 times with the sporicidal disinfectant, the exit chute and the reject bin are inspected, and the vials that passed through the affected part of the line since the last clean check are separated and held. The line clearance is repeated before filling resumes, and QA is told within 1 hour of the breakage so that it can decide on the batch.

### Vapour decontamination

The barrier and the filling room may be decontaminated with vapour after a shutdown, a barrier opening or an environmental excursion. The cycle parameters, including the concentration, exposure time and aeration limit, are taken from the vapour decontamination cycle report DCR-0026, and the operator does not alter them. The room is sealed before the cycle and sampling for residue is done before re-entry. A cycle that aborts is repeated in full rather than resumed, and the abort is recorded. The cycle report is reviewed every 12 months and after any change to the room or its equipment.

### Cleaning the parts washer and the autoclave

The equipment that cleans the parts has to be clean itself. The chamber of EQ-1310 is rinsed with WFI at the end of each day and the spray arms are checked for blocked nozzles every week. The concentration of detergent in the washer is checked by a conductivity reading every 7 days. The chamber of the autoclave EQ-1320 is wiped every day, its door gasket is inspected each week, and the drain strainer is cleared after every 5 cycles. Both machines are calibrated and maintained under SOP-EN-131 and the equipment maintenance plan.

## Records and review

### Cleaning records and status tags

Each piece of equipment carries a status tag stating whether it is dirty, clean or in use, the date and time of the last change of state, and the initials of the person who changed it. FRM-MF-301 is completed at each step, showing the agent, the lot of concentrate, the times and temperatures and the operator, and it is reviewed by the production supervisor within 1 working day. A tag that does not match the record is treated as an error, and the equipment is not used until the difference has been explained. Records are kept as set in POL-0004.

### Cleaning failures

A cleaning failure is any of the following: a swab result above the limit, a visible residue found after release, a hold time exceeded without re-cleaning, or a change to the method made without approval. A failure is reported the same shift and investigated under SOP-QA-101. The line is not used until the cause is understood and a repeat clean has passed. Where a pattern appears, for example the same swab site failing on 2 consecutive changeovers, the cleaning validation lead reviews the method.

### Trending of swab results

The QC analyst plots swab results by site for each product pair. A result above 80 percent of the 8 ug limit is treated as an alert and reported to the cleaning validation lead, even though it passes. Three consecutive results above 80 percent at one site trigger a review of the method, and a rising trend at a single site is discussed at the monthly quality meeting. The annual review of the programme compares the results for all sites and all product pairs and proposes changes to the swab plan.

### Training and observation

Operators clean unsupervised only after being trained on this procedure, on SPC-2260 and on the safe handling of the agents, and after 3 observed cleans signed off by a trained operator. Each operator is observed once every year in a routine clean, and the observer uses a checklist that follows the sequence in this procedure. An operator who is observed missing a step is retrained on that step before working alone again.
