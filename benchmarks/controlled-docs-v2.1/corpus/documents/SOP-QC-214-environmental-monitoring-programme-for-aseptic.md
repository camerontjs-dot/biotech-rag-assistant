# Environmental Monitoring Programme for Aseptic Suites

## 1. Purpose

This procedure describes how the microbiological and particle condition of the aseptic filling suites is monitored, recorded, reviewed and acted upon. It covers the choice of sampling locations, the preparation and handling of media, the use of the samplers and the particle counter, incubation and reading, and the response when a result crosses an alert or action level. The numerical limits, frequencies and incubation conditions are held in SPC-2231; this procedure says how to work to them.

## 2. Scope

The programme applies to the Grade A zone ZN-A1 inside barrier EQ-0602 on filling line FL-2, to the two Grade B rooms that surround it (RM-301, the filling room, and RM-304, stopper and closure preparation), to the gowning rooms RM-291 (Grade B) and RM-289 (Grade C), to the Grade C rooms RM-312 (formulation) and RM-318 (crimping), and to the Grade D rooms RM-325 (washing and staging) and RM-330 (materials airlock). Monitoring of operators' gloves and gowns follows SOP-QC-219. Water systems, stability chambers and cold storage are outside this procedure.

## 3. Definitions

Action level: a result at or above the value listed as the action limit in SPC-2231. It requires a documented response.

Alert level: an earlier warning value, lower than the action level, that signals drift and is trended.

Session: one continuous filling campaign on FL-2 from the start of the first fill to the end of the last, including breaks shorter than 30 minutes.

Recovery: any colony growing on a plate after incubation, whatever the organism.

Isolate: a distinct colony picked from a plate for identification.

Void plate: a plate that cannot be read reliably because it was dropped, left uncovered, cracked, over-dried or overgrown with spreading mould.

Location code: the short label that ties a sample to a mapped position, for example the fifth settle position in ZN-A1.

## Part A: Programme design

### 5. Basis of the programme

The sampling plan comes from a risk view of where contamination can reach the product: the closer a location is to open product, the more often and the more thoroughly it is sampled. Four factors carry most of the weight when a location is added or moved.

- Distance from open product and from the fill needle line.
- The number of people and the amount of movement nearby.
- Airflow direction, in particular whether the location sits downstream of a source.
- The recovery history of the location and of its neighbours.

Locations inside ZN-A1 are judged against the strictest limits, the Grade B rooms serve as the immediate background, and Grades C and D are sampled less often because they are further from open product. The plan is reviewed every 12 months, and earlier after a change to the line, a structural change to a room, a significant deviation, or a run of alert-level results that suggests the map no longer reflects the risk.

### 6. Review of the sampling plan

The sampling plan is reviewed at a meeting of the QC microbiology manager, the QC supervisor, a filling supervisor and a QA reviewer, held every 12 months and after any event that changes the risk picture. The meeting works from the monthly trend reports, the list of interventions with the highest recovery rates, and the list of locations with the most alert-level results in the year. It decides which locations to add, which to move and which to retire, and it records why. A location is retired only when it has produced no recovery in 24 months and no piece of equipment near it has changed. The revised map is issued within 10 working days of the meeting.

### 7. Room and zone classification

| Space | Grade | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ZN-A1 | A | Open-product zone inside EQ-0602 on FL-2 |
| RM-301 | B | Aseptic filling room, background to ZN-A1 |
| RM-304 | B | Stopper and closure preparation, transfer to the barrier |
| RM-291 | B | Final gowning room |
| RM-289 | C | First change room |
| RM-312 | C | Formulation suite |
| RM-318 | C | Crimping room |
| RM-325 | D | Washing and staging |
| RM-330 | D | Materials airlock |

A room keeps its grade only while its differential pressure, airflow pattern and recovery meet the qualification on file. A room that fails a qualification check is treated as one grade lower for monitoring purposes until it is released again.

### 8. Sampling locations

Locations are drawn on the site sampling map and labelled with a location code. Inside ZN-A1 the five settle positions are placed no more than 30 cm from the fill needle line, with the plate lid facing up and the plate clear of the stopper bowl. In RM-301 the six air locations include the door to RM-304, the transfer hatch, the nearest point to the barrier's glove ports, and the positions at the opposite wall. Contact sites are chosen to include product-adjacent surfaces, high-touch surfaces such as door handles and keypads, and at least one floor site near a drain zone. Every contact site on the map is sampled at least once in every 8 weeks, so that no position drops out of the rotation.

The gowning rooms are sampled because they are where operators are most exposed to the outside environment. In RM-291 the air location is placed beside the bench where the hood and mask are fitted, and the contact sites are the bench top, the mirror frame and the exit door handle. When equipment is added to a room, or a piece is moved, the analyst proposes new locations to the QC supervisor before first use, and the map is reissued within 10 working days of the change.

### 9. Media and plate preparation

TSA plates (MAT-2231 for settle exposure and MAT-2233 for contact sampling) are used for total aerobic count, and SDA plates (MAT-2236) for fungal recovery in the Grade B rooms. Where a surface has just been disinfected, the contact plate with neutralizer (MAT-2240) replaces MAT-2233. Plates are received double-bagged, stored at 2 to 8 degrees C and inspected for cracks, dehydration and colour change before use. Each new lot passes a growth promotion test against at least 3 challenge organisms and a sterility check before it is released for monitoring. Plate shelf life and the use-by rule for irradiated plates are set in SPC-2231. A plate that has warmed to room temperature for more than 2 hours before exposure is discarded rather than returned to the refrigerator.

The outer bag is opened only in the pass-through of RM-330 or in the room where the plates will be used. Sleeves are never opened in the QC laboratory and carried in, because the outside of the sleeve has been handled and stored in a non-classified space.

## Part B: Sampling methods

### 10. Sampler set-up and verification

Before each filling session the analyst checks the sampler head, wipes the outer casing with sterile 70 percent isopropyl alcohol, and confirms with the flow meter EQ-0850 that the flow is 100 L/min within plus or minus 5 percent. A sample of 1000 L therefore takes 10 minutes. The head is loaded with a fresh plate inside the room, in the same gowning as the operators, and the sampler is placed at the mapped position with the intake at working height. If the flow check fails twice, the sampler is tagged and the second sampler is used.

### 11. Settle plate exposure

Settle plates are uncovered when the first fill starts and covered again at the end of the exposure period. The exposure limit is given in SPC-2231. The analyst notes the uncover time and the cover time on FRM-QC-117 and initials both entries. A plate that has to be replaced during a session because of a spill or contact is logged with the reason, and the replacement carries the same location code with a suffix. Plates are never exposed on a stand that could block unidirectional airflow, and they are placed after the operators have completed their final gowning check, never before.

### 12. Active air sampling

Active air samples in Grade B are taken during activity, not at rest. The analyst walks a fixed route so that the sampler never passes close to open product, starts the sampler from the outside of the barrier where possible, and does not lean over the plate during sampling. Between locations the head is disinfected with sterile 70 percent isopropyl alcohol and allowed to dry for at least 30 seconds. A sample interrupted by an alarm is repeated in full at the same location rather than combined with a partial count.

### 13. Contact plate sampling

Contact plates are pressed against the surface with even pressure for 10 seconds and lifted straight off without sliding. After sampling, the surface is wiped with sterile 70 percent isopropyl alcohol to remove agar residue, because residue can support growth and would itself be a source of contamination. Surfaces that have been disinfected are sampled only after the dwell period has elapsed, as fixed in the disinfectant efficacy study DES-0032, and the neutralizer plate is used in that case. Curved or vertical surfaces are sampled with the plate rolled once, not pressed twice.

### 14. Non-viable particle monitoring

The particle counter EQ-0417 samples continuously from the probe inside ZN-A1 during operation. The counter's alarm thresholds follow the limits in SPC-2231. A particle alarm during an intervention is logged with the time and the intervention it coincides with, and the count is reviewed with the intervention log at the end of the session. The interval at which the counter itself is recalibrated comes from the counter qualification report QRP-0417. Before each session the analyst confirms that the counter's zero count check has passed and that the isokinetic probe is clean and correctly positioned. The zero count check runs for 3 minutes with a filter on the inlet, and a counter that records any particle in that time is not used for the session.

### 15. Sampling for new products and new line configurations

Before a new product, a new vial size or a new line configuration is run for the first time, QC and production agree the sampling plan for it. The plan starts from the standard frequency in SPC-2231 and adds 2 settle positions and 2 contact sites in ZN-A1 for the first 3 sessions, so that any effect of the new configuration on the environment is seen early. Locations that the change makes hard to reach are replaced by the nearest reachable position, and the reason is written on the map. The extra sampling ends only when the QC microbiology manager has reviewed the results of the 3 sessions and signed the plan back to the standard frequency.

### 16. Labelling and transport

Every plate carries a printed label with the location code, the date, the start time and the analyst's initials, and the stop time is added by hand. Plates are transported in a closed insulated box, held between 15 and 25 degrees C, and travel from RM-330 to the QC laboratory by the shortest route without stopping. Boxes are never left on a warm surface or beside equipment that gives off heat. The time limit between collection and incubation is set in SPC-2231. Plates that miss that limit are recorded as a handling failure and the location is sampled again.

### 17. Plates during weekends and holidays

Filling sessions that end on a Friday, or before a public holiday, produce plates that must still be read on the scheduled day. The QC supervisor keeps an on-call roster, and the analyst on call reads plates on the day the final reading falls due, whether or not it is a working day. An incubator alarm outside working hours is answered by the on-call technician within 30 minutes, and the plates inside are recorded as affected only if the temperature was outside the required range for more than 60 minutes. Plates are never left unread over a weekend to be counted on Monday, since colonies that run together after the due day cannot be counted reliably.

## Part C: Incubation, reading and review

### 18. Incubation and reading

TSA and SDA plates are incubated under the conditions in SPC-2231 in the incubators EQ-0421 and EQ-0422. The incubator temperature is read and initialled on each working day, and a reading outside the required range is reported to the QC supervisor at once, together with the list of plates that were inside during the excursion. Plates are looked at after 3 days for early mould and read finally at the end of the incubation period. Colonies are counted with the colony counter; a plate with more than 150 colonies is recorded as too numerous to count and treated as an action-level result. A plate that has been read must not be returned to the incubator for further growth.

When a plate shows more than one colony type, the analyst records the count for each morphology separately as well as the total, because the total decides the level of the result while the split guides identification. Spreading mould is counted as one colony where it can be traced to a single origin and is otherwise voided.

### 19. Worked reading of a trend

The following example shows how the trend rule and the monthly review fit together. In RM-301 the third air location gave a Grade B result of 3 CFU per m3 on the first Monday of a month, 4 CFU per m3 on the Thursday of the same week and 3 CFU per m3 on the following Tuesday. Each result was at the alert level and below the action level, so none needed a deviation, but three alert-level results at one location in 8 days met the trend rule and an investigation was opened. The investigator found that a door closer near that location had been replaced 9 days earlier and was no longer closing fully, which allowed air to move between RM-301 and the corridor. The closer was adjusted, the location was sampled at each session for the next 2 weeks, and the trend investigation was closed after 6 consecutive sessions without an alert.

### 20. Recording and second-person review

The analyst records each result on FRM-QC-117 at the time of the reading, never from memory or from a loose note. A second person reviews the completed record within 2 working days of the final plate read, checking location codes, exposure times, plate lots, the incubator used and the arithmetic of any conversions. Corrections are made with a single line, initials, date and reason. The reviewer confirms that any alert or action level has been carried through to the trending file and, for action levels, to the response record.

## Part D: Responding to results

### 21. Alert and action level response

An alert-level result is recorded and trended, and the analyst checks whether the same location is close to the trend rule in SPC-2231. An action-level result in Grade A or Grade B is reported to the QC supervisor within 2 hours of the plate being read, and a deviation is raised under SOP-QA-101 without waiting for the identification result. The response is written on FRM-QC-121.

1. Confirm the reading with a second analyst.
2. Notify the QC supervisor, the filling supervisor and, for Grade A, the QA reviewer.
3. Review the session log for interventions, line stops and door openings near the location.
4. Sample the location again on the next working day.
5. Send the isolate for identification on FRM-QC-131.
6. Record the outcome and any product impact on FRM-QC-121.

Action-level results in Grade C and Grade D are reported to the QC supervisor on the same working day and handled through FRM-QC-121 without an automatic deviation, unless the same location has shown action-level results before or the organism is one that identification has flagged as objectionable.

### 22. Investigation of a recurring recovery

A recovery that returns to the same location, or the same organism turning up in two or more locations, is investigated rather than simply re-sampled. The investigator gathers the differential pressure records for the room over the previous 30 days, the gowning session log, the cleaning and disinfection record from SOP-MF-207, the maintenance work orders for the area and the intervention log for the sessions concerned. Each source is checked for a change that lines up with the first recovery: a door left open, a pressure alarm, a new disinfectant lot, a repaired seal, a different operator team. The conclusion names the most probable source, states how confident the investigator is, and lists what will be sampled to confirm it. A source that cannot be found is recorded as such and the location stays on a raised sampling frequency until three consecutive sessions are clean.

### 23. Isolate identification

Which isolates are identified, and how quickly the result is reported for Grade A and Grade B, is set in SPC-2231. For Grade C and Grade D isolates that fall under the identification rule, the turnaround follows the period agreed in the service agreement SLA-0007 with the identification laboratory, and the analyst records the expected return date on FRM-QC-131. Isolates from Grade A and Grade B locations are kept at minus 20 degrees C in glycerol stock for 24 months, so that a later recovery can be compared with earlier ones. Identification results are entered into the isolate library, where organism, location, grade and date are searchable. A repeat isolate from the same location within a month is compared with the library entry and flagged if it matches a previous recovery.

### 24. Trending and reporting

The trend rule that starts an investigation from alert-level results is in SPC-2231. Beyond that rule, the QC microbiology manager reviews charts for each grade every month: recovery rates by location, by session and by shift, and the proportion of results at alert level. A monthly trend report goes to QA by the 10th working day of the following month. The programme as a whole, including the sampling map and the limits, is reviewed every 12 months, and the review is signed by the QC microbiology manager and the head of QA.

## Part E: Supporting controls

### 25. Product-contact gases

Compressed air that reaches ZN-A1 is checked for viable organisms once a month at 4 points, sampling 1000 L at each, and the action limit is 1 CFU per m3. The nitrogen manifold is checked once a quarter at its outlets. The alert level for the nitrogen check follows the gas supply specification GSP-0015, and any result is trended against the earlier results from the same outlet. A gas result above the action limit is treated as an action-level result in Grade A.

### 26. Alarm limits and responses for the particle counter

The particle counter is set with alarm thresholds at the alert and action levels in SPC-2231. An alert alarm during operation is logged and reviewed at the end of the session. An action alarm is handled as an intervention: the filling supervisor is told at once, the count is watched for 5 minutes, and if the count does not return below the action level within that time the operator on the line pauses filling at the next safe point. The pause and its cause are written on FRM-QC-121, and filling restarts only when the QC supervisor has confirmed that the counts are back within limits for 5 continuous minutes.

### 27. Occupancy and interventions

The number of people in each room while filling is limited. RM-301 holds no more than 5 people during filling, and RM-304 no more than 3. The filling supervisor logs every intervention in ZN-A1, and the log is compared with the plate results at the end of each session to look for a link between an intervention and a recovery. During a media fill simulation the settle plates and the contact sites are handled exactly as in production so that the simulation is representative. Extra personnel, such as engineers or auditors, are counted against the room limit and gowned to the same standard as the operators.

### 28. Monitoring after shutdowns and maintenance

After a line shutdown longer than 14 days, or after maintenance that opened the barrier or the ceiling of a Grade B room, the suite is sampled at rest on 3 consecutive days before filling restarts, using the frequency and locations that apply to a filling session. At-rest results are judged against the same action levels as in operation. The QC supervisor signs the release to fill on FRM-QC-121, and the release records the disinfectant cycle that was completed before the first at-rest sample was taken. Short stops, such as a stopper bowl change or a vial jam clearance, do not trigger this extra sampling.

### 29. Sampler unavailability

If neither sampler EQ-0412 nor EQ-0413 is serviceable, active air sampling is not skipped silently. With QA approval, one filling session may go ahead using settle plates only, and the approval is recorded on FRM-QC-121. A second session without a working sampler is not permitted; the line waits until a sampler is repaired, replaced or borrowed from a qualified alternative. The Engineering technician logs the fault and the return to service, and QC repeats the flow check before the sampler is used again.

### 30. Analyst qualification

A monitoring analyst is qualified before working unsupervised and is requalified every 12 months. Requalification is a reading test on 10 blind plates, and the analyst must count at least 9 of them correctly and identify the presence of mould where it is present. The qualification also covers aseptic sampling technique, observed by a qualified analyst, and gowning to the standard in SOP-TR-021. An analyst who fails the test is removed from independent sampling until retrained and retested.

### 31. Equipment maintenance and calibration

Samplers, the flow meter, the particle counter and the incubators are maintained under the calibration and maintenance programme of SOP-EN-131. The HEPA filter on each sampler's exhaust is replaced every 12 months, and the sampler is disinfected before it re-enters a Grade B room. The incubators are checked with a calibrated thermometer each working day and mapped when they are moved. A piece of equipment past its due date is tagged out and not used for monitoring.

## Part F: Practice and records

### 32. Typical problems and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Growth on a plate only at its edge | Lid lifted during transport | Record as a handling failure, sample again |
| Spreading mould covering the plate | Damaged plate, or lid left off | Void the plate, sample again the same day |
| Sampler shows 92 L/min | Filter loaded or hose kinked | Tag the sampler, use EQ-0413 |
| Dry, cracked agar | Plate warmed or stored too long | Discard the lot section, review storage |
| Blurred label | Label printed before plates were chilled | Reprint and re-label with a witness |
| Two locations show the same isolate | Common source, such as a shared tool | Open a trend investigation |
| Particle alarm with no matching intervention | Probe tubing bent or loose | Check the tubing, repeat the zero count |

### 33. Objectionable organisms

The QC microbiology manager keeps a short list of organisms that are treated as objectionable wherever they are found in Grade A or Grade B, because of what they can do to a product or what they say about the state of the suite. The list covers spore-forming bacteria, organisms associated with water or with people that are unusual for the location, and any mould recovered from ZN-A1. A recovery on that list is reported to the QC supervisor immediately, whatever its count, and the filling supervisor is told to hold the line at the next safe point until the QA reviewer has decided whether filling may continue. The decision and its reasoning are written on FRM-QC-121, and the list is reviewed as part of the annual programme review.

### 34. Data integrity and plate images

Each plate is photographed at the final read with its label visible, and the image is attached to the electronic record of FRM-QC-117. The photograph does not replace the count: the analyst's count is the result, and the image is there so that a reviewer or an investigator can see what the analyst saw. Images are stored in the controlled record system, never on a personal device or a shared drive, and they are named with the location code, the date and the plate lot so that they can be retrieved without opening every record. A count that is later found to disagree with its image is investigated as a data integrity concern, not corrected quietly.

### 35. Communication with production

Filling supervisors receive a short status each morning listing any alert or action-level results read the previous day and any location under raised sampling. The status is a summary from the trending file, not a new record, and it never quotes a result that has not been through second-person review. Requests from production to move a sampling position, to skip a location because of an intervention, or to change the plate lot are made to the QC supervisor and are answered in writing; the analyst does not agree to changes at the line. Production may ask for extra sampling at any time, and a request for extra sampling is always granted.

### 36. Records and retention

Completed FRM-QC-117, FRM-QC-121, FRM-QC-126 and FRM-QC-131 entries, the isolate library, incubator logs and trend reports are kept for the periods set in POL-0004. Raw plate counts are recorded on the form at the time of reading and are never transcribed into a spreadsheet without the second-person check described under recording and review.
