# Product Complaint Handling

## Purpose

A product complaint is any report that a product is not as it should be, from a patient, a clinician, a pharmacist, a distributor or anyone else who has used or handled it. Complaints are one of the few signals that reach the site from outside the plant, and this procedure sets out how they are received, classified, investigated, answered and used to improve the product. It applies to all marketed products and to product supplied for clinical use.

## What counts as a complaint

A complaint may concern the appearance of the product, particles or discolouration, the container or closure, labelling, a suspected mix-up, an unexpected reaction, a lack of effect, damage in transit or the temperature history of a shipment. An enquiry that asks only for information is not a complaint, but it is logged if it reveals a possible quality problem. Anyone at the site who hears of a complaint records it and passes it on within the same working day.

## Intake

The customer quality officer logs each complaint on FRM-QA-601 on the day it is received, and the record receives a number in the form CM-nnnn. The form captures who reported it, the product code and lot, the nature of the complaint, the date and place of use where known, and whether a sample is available. Where the reporter does not know the lot, the officer asks for the carton or the label and records what could be established. A complaint that arrives by several routes, for example by phone and by email, is linked to a single record. A call that is missed during working hours is returned within 30 minutes. A call received outside working hours is logged within 12 hours, the message box is checked every 2 hours in working time, and a response letter is proofread by a second person in no more than 1 hour.

## Acknowledgement

The complainant is sent an acknowledgement within 5 working days of receipt. It confirms that the complaint has been logged, gives the reference, says what will happen next and asks for the sample if one is available. The acknowledgement does not draw a conclusion about the cause and does not promise an outcome. The customer quality officer records the date and the method of contact.

## Classification

The customer quality officer proposes a class within 2 working days of receipt, and the QA reviewer confirms it.

| Class | Meaning | Investigation completed within |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Critical | Potential or actual harm to a patient, or a suspected mix-up, contamination or falsification | 25 calendar days |
| Major | Possible effect on the quality or the safe use of the product, without evidence of harm | 45 calendar days |
| Minor | Cosmetic or handling matter with no effect on quality or safety | 75 calendar days |

Where the class is uncertain, the higher class applies until the investigation shows otherwise, except that a complaint about product more than 6 months past its expiry is classified Minor unless harm is reported. Complaint samples are retained for 12 months after the investigation closes. The class can be changed as facts emerge, and the change and the reason are recorded.

## Critical complaints

A Critical complaint is escalated to the QA Head within 24 hours of being classified, by telephone and in writing, and the escalation is recorded. On the same working day, QA assesses whether the batch involved may be affected, places related stock on hold under POL-0012 where that is justified, and starts the assessment of whether an outside authority must be told. The QA Head or a named deputy can be reached 24 hours a day, and the on-call number is posted at the customer quality desk. That assessment is completed within 3 working days of classification and is written on the complaint record.

## Complaint samples and retained samples

Where the complainant has the product, a sample is requested within 3 working days of receipt, and the complainant is asked to return it within 21 days. The returned sample is logged on receipt, stored in quarantine at 2 to 8 degrees C unless the label states another condition, and never discarded before the investigation is closed. If the sample has not arrived after 14 days a reminder is sent, a second reminder follows at 28 days, and the request lapses after 60 days with the lapse recorded. The retained samples of the lot kept by QA are checked within 5 working days of the complaint, to see whether the defect can be found in material that never left the site. Photographs of a returned unit are taken before any test and are kept with the record, and their retention period follows the product retention plan PRP-0007.

## Investigation

The investigation is led by a QA investigator and uses FRM-QA-604. It reviews the batch record, the deviations and the environmental monitoring for the lot, the results of the retained samples and the return sample, the complaint history for the product and the lot, and the supply route where the temperature history is in question. It tests the sample by the appropriate methods and states whether the complaint is confirmed, not confirmed or cannot be determined. A finding of not confirmed is supported by evidence and not simply by the absence of a defect in a single sample. QA reviews every open complaint each 7 days, and every Critical complaint each working day until it is closed.

## Product impact and batch disposition

Where the investigation confirms a defect that could affect other units, QA assesses the whole lot and other lots made under similar conditions. A released batch whose quality is in question has its disposition file reopened under SOP-QA-133 and the release authority decides on holds, recalls or further testing. A decision to recall is escalated to the QA Head at once and handled under the site's recall arrangements.

## Corrective and preventive action

A confirmed complaint is examined for its root cause, and where the cause is systemic, a CAPA is raised under SOP-QA-172. Where the complaint exposes a deviation that had not been reported, one is raised under SOP-QA-101. A CAPA that comes from a complaint keeps a link to the complaint number, so that the answer to the complainant and the action can be traced to each other.

## Response to the complainant

A written response goes to the complainant within 10 working days of the closure of the investigation. It states what was found, in language the complainant can follow, and what has been done about it. Where the complaint is not confirmed, the response says what was examined and why the finding was reached. A response to a healthcare professional includes the contact of the medical information team where relevant. Complainants in Critical cases are also telephoned within 2 days of the closure, so that the letter is not the first they hear of the outcome.

## Duplicate and related complaints

A second complaint on the same lot is linked to the first record, and the classification is reviewed whenever another arrives, since two similar complaints may be more serious than either alone. A cluster of 3 or more complaints about the same defect within 30 days is reported to the QA Head and reviewed at the next quality meeting.

## Trending

Every quarter, the customer quality officer reports complaints by product, lot, type and class, with rates per 1,000,000 units supplied. The QA Head reviews the report with the department heads, and the trend for each type is compared with the previous 4 quarters. Where a type is rising, an investigation of the type as a whole is opened even when each single complaint has been closed. The annual complaint review covers the previous 12 months and is completed within 60 days of the year end. A complaint that has stayed open for more than 90 days is reported to the QA Head every month until it is closed. Duplicates are searched for in the records of the previous 180 days.

## Returns

Product that comes back from a customer with a complaint is treated as a complaint sample. Product returned for other reasons follows SOP-QA-151. The visual examination of returned units follows the assessment form FRM-QA-610.

## Records

Complaint records, samples, photographs, investigations and responses are retained as set in POL-0004 and are reviewed at inspection and at the annual quality review. Personal data of complainants are handled in accordance with the site's data protection rules and shared only with those who need them for the investigation.
