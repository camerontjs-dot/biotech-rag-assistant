# Stability Sample Handling Note

## What this note covers

This note is for chamber technicians and analysts who load, pull and test stability samples. It sets out what you do with a unit from the moment it is labelled to the moment it reaches the bench, and the numbers that matter most on a pull day. The controlling documents are SOP-QC-236, SPC-2271 and TCN-4110, and this note follows them in simplified form.

## Labels and loading

Check every label before a unit goes into a chamber: product code, lot, condition, pull point and unit number must all be legible. Ask a colleague to check them again. Load the units in the order on the loading list, write the shelf on FRM-QC-301 and do not take longer than an hour with the chamber door open in total. If a chamber is filling up, ask the coordinator for the backup chamber instead of stacking units. Every quarter the coordinator counts each chamber against the inventory on FRM-QC-310, so a unit that is not on the loading list will be found.

## Pull windows

A pull must happen close to its nominal date. The window for the 3-month and 6-month pulls is plus or minus 14 days, the window for 9 and 12 months is plus or minus 21 days, and from 18 months onward it is plus or minus 30 days. Do not leave a pull to the last day of the window; plan it for the middle. If a pull will miss its window, tell the coordinator before the window closes so that it can be logged properly.

## On the day of a pull

1. Read the pull request and check the units named on it.
2. Open the chamber, remove exactly those units and close the door.
3. Check the labels against the request and note the time.
4. Carry the units in the closed tray, out of light and heat, to the laboratory.

Samples must reach the laboratory within 6 hours of leaving the chamber. If something delays you, tell the analyst, who will decide whether the samples are still usable. Never leave pulled units on a bench while you finish another job.

## Chamber checks

The monitoring system EQ-1410 records temperature and humidity every 15 minutes. Look at the display when you open a chamber and at the trace at the start of each shift. An alarm goes first to the on-call technician, so if you hear one, find out who has acknowledged it before you do anything else. The chambers are mapped every 24 months and after any move, so if a chamber has been relocated, ask whether the mapping has been done before you load it.

## When a result looks different

The analyst checks each new result against the earlier ones for the same batch. An assay that has fallen by more than 2.5 percent since the previous time point is an alert and goes to the coordinator on the day it is known. Do not repeat a test to see whether the number comes back; the coordinator decides what happens next.

## Reserve units and leftovers

Reserve units stay in the chamber until the results are accepted. After the last pull of a study, units that remain are kept for 12 months. Do not throw anything away without a signed record, and do not take a unit for an informal check.

## Mistakes seen most often

- Removing units that were not on the pull request.
- Writing the removal time on a glove or a scrap of paper instead of the form.
- Opening a chamber for a long conversation with the door ajar.
- Moving a unit to a different shelf without noting it.
- Testing a unit that has no label.
