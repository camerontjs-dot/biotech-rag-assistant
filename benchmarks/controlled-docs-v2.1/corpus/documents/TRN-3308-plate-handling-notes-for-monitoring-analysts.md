# Plate Handling Notes for Monitoring Analysts

## Who this note is for

New monitoring analysts in their first eight weeks, and anyone who has been away from sampling for more than a quarter. It is a working companion to SOP-QC-214 and SPC-2231, which remain the controlling documents. Read it beside the plates, not instead of the procedure.

## Before you go in

Check that the plates in your tray are the right type for the job. Green-labelled MAT-2231 plates are for settle exposure, MAT-2233 is the 55 mm contact plate, MAT-2236 is the SDA plate for fungal recovery, and MAT-2240 is the contact plate with neutralizer that you use on a surface that has just been disinfected. Look for cracks, dry edges and any change in colour, and put any doubtful plate back in the tray. You should carry more plates than you think you need; a spare is far better than a gap in the record.

Confirm that sampler EQ-0412 is showing 100 L/min before you carry it into RM-301. If it does not, use EQ-0413 and tell the QC supervisor.

## Settle plates

Take the lid off when filling starts and put it back on at the end of the run. A settle plate should never be open longer than 2.5 hours, so in a long run you stop, cover the first plate and put out a fresh one. Write the uncover and cover times on FRM-QC-117 straight away; do not rely on remembering them. In ZN-A1 keep the plate within reach of the fill needle line and clear of the stopper bowl.

## Air samples

Take 1000 L at each mapped location. That is ten minutes at the correct flow. Walk the same route each time, do not stand over the plate, and wipe the head with sterile 70 percent isopropyl alcohol between locations. If the sampler alarms, repeat the whole sample at that location.

## Contact plates

Press the plate against the surface for 10 seconds with even pressure, lift it straight off, and wipe the surface afterwards so no agar is left behind. For a curved surface roll the plate once. Contact plates in Grade B rooms show an alert at 4 colonies and an action at 8 colonies on a single plate, so anything at 4 or more is worth flagging to your supervisor even before the plates are read.

## Getting plates to the incubator

Carry plates back in the closed insulated box, keep the box level, and take the shortest route. Plates should be in the incubator within 8 hours of collection, and if you are running late you should tell the supervisor rather than hurry and tip the box. Put TSA plates in EQ-0421 at 30 to 35 degrees C and leave them for 3 days. SDA plates go into EQ-0422 at 20 to 25 degrees C for 5 days. Look at the plates after 3 days for early mould before they are read.

## Reading and recording

Count the colonies on each plate with the colony counter and record the number on FRM-QC-117 before you do anything else with the plate. If a plate has more than 150 colonies write TNTC and call the supervisor. Once you have read a plate, it is finished: never put a read plate back in the incubator to see whether it grows more.

## What the numbers mean in Grade B air

A Grade B air result of 5 colonies per m3 is an alert and a result of 13 colonies per m3 or more is an action. When you see a result at or above the action level, tell the QC supervisor within 2 hours of reading the plate, and ask a second analyst to confirm the count before you leave the laboratory.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Opening plate sleeves in the QC laboratory rather than in the room where the plates will be used.
- Leaving the box on top of the incubator, where the surface is warm.
- Writing the stop time from memory at the end of the shift.
- Reading a plate that has not been labelled and guessing the location.
- Putting a used plate back into the incubator after it has been counted.
