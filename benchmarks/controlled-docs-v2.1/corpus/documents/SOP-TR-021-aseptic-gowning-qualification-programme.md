# Aseptic Gowning Qualification Programme

## Purpose

People shed particles and organisms continuously, and the gown is the barrier between the operator and the product. This procedure sets out how personnel are qualified to gown for and work in the classified aseptic areas, how their qualification is maintained, and what happens when it lapses or fails. It links training, observation and microbiological sampling into one programme, so that nobody works in a Grade B room or in ZN-A1 without evidence that they can gown and behave to the required standard.

## Scope

The programme covers all employees, contractors and trainees who enter the Grade B rooms RM-301, RM-304 and RM-291, the Grade C rooms RM-289, RM-312 and RM-318, the Grade D rooms RM-325 and RM-330, and the Grade A zone ZN-A1 inside barrier EQ-0602. Qualification is granted by grade: an operator qualified for Grade B may also work in Grades C and D, but an operator qualified only for Grade C or D may not enter Grade B. Visitors are covered by their own section below.

## Definitions

Gowning session: one complete change from street clothes to the required cleanroom garments, from entry into RM-289 to exit from the final gowning room RM-291.

Qualification: the documented result of a set of successful gowning sessions, with sampling and observation, that permits an operator to work in a grade.

Requalification: the repeat of the qualification at the required interval.

Observer: a qualified person who watches a gowning session and completes the observer checklist FRM-TR-105.

Stand-down: a period during which an operator is withdrawn from a grade after repeated failure.

Qualification lapse: the loss of qualification because the time limits in this procedure have been passed.

## Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Operator | Gowns as trained, follows behaviour rules, reports illness and skin conditions |

| QC microbiology analyst | Takes and reads gowning samples, reports results |

| QA reviewer | Reviews failures, approves exceptions, audits the programme |

## Preparing to gown

### Eligibility and health screening

An employee is eligible to start the qualification only after completing the site's general GMP induction of 2 days and a medical screening arranged by occupational health. The screening looks for conditions that would make gowned work unsafe or would increase shedding, such as chronic skin disease or a respiratory condition that cannot be controlled. The period for which a medical clearance remains valid follows the occupational health procedure OHP-0004, and the qualification coordinator checks the clearance date before scheduling any session. Employees with a temporary condition are deferred, not rejected, and are rebooked when the condition has resolved.

### Principles of aseptic behaviour

The garments are only one part of the barrier; the operator's behaviour decides whether the barrier holds. Operators move slowly and deliberately, because fast movement releases more particles from the body and disturbs the airflow. They avoid touching the face, the hood and the gloves' outer surface except as the task needs. They do not talk over open product, and they keep arms and hands below or beside, never above, the open containers. They plan their movements so as to keep the number of entries and exits low, and they leave the room at the first sign that a garment has been damaged. These principles are taught, observed and assessed, and they are the reason that a technically clean gown does not on its own qualify an operator.

### The gowning rooms and flow

Operators enter through the first change room RM-289 (Grade C), where street clothes and personal items are removed and a clean undergarment is put on. They then move through an interlocked doorway to the final gowning room RM-291 (Grade B), where the sterile garments are put on. Movement is one way: the exit from the Grade B area is through a separate route, so that gowned operators do not meet operators who are entering. Benches in RM-291 separate the side for donning from the side for storage, and a mirror is provided so that the operator can check the completed gown.

### Garments and materials

The sterile garment pack MAT-6010 contains a coverall, a hood, a face mask, boot covers and a pair of goggles, and the sterile gloves are supplied separately as MAT-6015. Packs are stored in a closed cabinet in RM-291, and the operator takes the oldest pack. A pack that is damaged, wet or open is discarded, and each pack carries a label with the number of sterilisation and laundering cycles the garments have completed. The validation limit for the sterilisation of the garments comes from the certificate of the gowning supplier GSC-0003.

### The gowning sequence

The sequence has 14 steps, and it is followed in order every time. The observer uses it as the checklist for an observed session.

1. Remove street clothes and personal items and put on the clean undergarment in RM-289.
2. Put on the first boot covers and step across the bench to the clean side.
3. Sanitise hands for the required time.
4. Open the garment pack without touching the outside of the inner packaging.
5. Put on the hood so that all hair is covered.
6. Fit the face mask over the nose and mouth.
7. Put on the goggles.
8. Step into the coverall without letting it touch the floor.
9. Fasten the coverall from the bottom to the top.
10. Tuck the hood into the collar of the coverall.
11. Put on the second boot covers over the trouser cuffs.
12. Put on the sterile gloves over the cuffs.
13. Sanitise the gloves.
14. Check the whole gown in the mirror and confirm that no skin is exposed.

### Hand sanitisation

Hands are sanitised with 70 percent isopropyl alcohol for 30 seconds before the garment pack is opened and again after the gloves are put on. The operator rubs the alcohol over every surface of the hands and fingers, including the thumbs and the fingertips, and does not use paper towels or dryers. Gloves are sanitised in the same way, and the operator waits until they are dry before touching any surface. A session in which the sanitisation time is shortened or skipped is recorded as an observer finding.

## Qualification sessions

### Observer qualification

Observers are drawn from operators who have held Grade B qualification for at least 12 months without a failure. An observer completes 4 hours of observer training, which covers the sequence, the common breaches and how to record a finding without coaching, and then watches 3 sessions under the supervision of an experienced observer before working alone. Observers are reassessed every 24 months. An observer who has a session of their own scheduled cannot observe on the same day, and an observer never watches an operator who reports to them directly, so that the observation stays independent.

### Observed sessions

Every qualification session is watched by a qualified observer who does not help the operator, and who records the order of steps, the time taken and any contact between the outside of the gown and any surface. The observer stops the session if a serious breach occurs, for example a glove that touches the floor. The observer signs FRM-TR-105 at the end and hands it to the qualification coordinator. An operator is not told the sampling results until the observer has completed the checklist.

### Timing and preparation of sessions

Qualification sessions are scheduled between shifts, when the gowning rooms are quiet, and are allowed 45 minutes each. The operator arrives with no personal items, has eaten at least 30 minutes before and has not used cosmetics or hand cream on the day. The QC analyst prepares the plates before the session and checks that the incubators are within range. A session is not started if the observer, the analyst or the room is not ready, since delays during a session change the conditions that the sampling is meant to reflect.

### Sampling at qualification

After the gowning is complete, and before the operator moves or touches anything, the QC analyst samples the forearm of the dominant arm, the centre of the chest and the hood at the level of the forehead, using 55 mm contact plates (MAT-2233), and takes glove prints of both hands. The operator's identifier is recorded on every plate. The plates are handled, incubated and read as described in SOP-QC-214 and SOP-QC-219, and the counts are entered on FRM-TR-101.

### Reading results at the limit

A count is compared with the limit as it stands: 3 CFU per plate at a gown site is acceptable, and 4 CFU per plate is not. Glove prints must show no growth at all. All counts are recorded, including those that pass, and a count at the limit at any site is noted for the coordinator, who watches for operators whose sites are repeatedly at the limit. Plates that cannot be read because of spreading mould or damage are voided, and the session is repeated on another day; a voided plate is never counted as a pass.

### Acceptance criteria

A gowning session is successful when the observer's checklist shows no critical finding and the sampling gives no growth on either glove print and a count of 3 CFU per plate or fewer at each of the three gown sites. A single plate over the limit makes the session unsuccessful. An operator becomes qualified when 3 consecutive sessions are successful, taken on separate days, and the qualification coordinator enters the date of the third success as the date of qualification.

### Failure and retraining

An operator whose session is unsuccessful is withdrawn from the grade and given at least 4 hours of retraining on the points that failed before the next attempt. The retraining is recorded on FRM-TR-110. The count of consecutive successes returns to zero after a failure. An operator who fails twice within 30 days is stood down from Grade B for 30 days, during which the operator may work in Grades C and D only if qualified for them, and then restarts the qualification from the first session.

### Requalification

Operators working in Grade B are requalified every 6 months, and operators working in Grades C and D every 12 months. A requalification is a single session with observation and sampling, judged by the same acceptance criteria as the initial qualification. A failed requalification is treated like any failure. The qualification coordinator schedules the session within the 4 weeks before the due date, and the area supervisor releases the operator for it.

## Training and practical assessment

### Assessment of the practical training

The practical assessment is scored out of 20 points, of which each step of the gowning sequence and each behaviour rule carries a weight. The pass mark is 18 points. Any critical breach, such as a glove touching the floor, an exposed patch of skin or an unsterile item entering the barrier zone, fails the assessment whatever the score. A candidate who fails may repeat the assessment after retraining, and the second failure is reported to the area supervisor and to the qualification coordinator for a decision on the candidate's role.

### Training programme

Before the first qualification session, an operator completes 16 hours of classroom training and 8 hours of practical training. The classroom part covers the sources of contamination, the principles of aseptic behaviour, the gowning sequence, the classification of the rooms, and the meaning of the environmental monitoring results in SOP-QC-214. The practical part takes place in a training suite with an experienced operator, and covers the whole gowning sequence, movement in the gowning rooms and the handling of the garments and gloves. Assessment is by written test and a practical demonstration, and the pass mark and the record follow SOP-TR-026.

## Gowning in practice and the Grade A zone

### Interlocked doors

The doors between RM-289 and RM-291 are interlocked, so that only one can be opened at a time, and the second door will not release until 15 seconds after the first has closed. The delay lets the air in the airlock change and reduces the transfer of particles from the change room. Operators do not hold a door open or prop it, and any failure of the interlock is reported to Engineering the same shift and the door pair is taken out of use until it is repaired.

### Double gloving and donning technique

Operators who work in ZN-A1 wear two pairs of sterile gloves: an inner pair that stays on for the whole session, and an outer pair that is changed at the interval below. The inner pair is put on in RM-291 and sanitised, and the outer pair is put on at the transfer position so that its outside is never touched by anything but the sterile inner glove of the other hand. The technique of donning uses the folded cuff of each glove and never the outside surface. Sizes are chosen at first qualification, since a glove that is too loose makes movements clumsy, and a glove that is too tight tears sooner.

### Gloves worn in ZN-A1

An operator who works in ZN-A1 replaces the outer sterile gloves after 2 hours of continuous wear, and after any visible damage, any suspected contact with a non-sterile surface and any intervention that involves the floor of the barrier. The change is made at the transfer position with the gloves sanitised, and the time of each change is written on the session log. Gloves are inspected for holes by stretching before use, and a pair with any hole is discarded.

### Qualification for the Grade A zone

Qualification for Grade B is the first step, and work in ZN-A1 needs more. An operator must have worked in Grade B for at least 3 months, must have been qualified on the list of interventions in the media fill protocol, and must have completed 1 observed session in the simulator suite that reproduces the barrier and the glove ports. The QA reviewer signs the extension of the qualification to ZN-A1, and the register shows it as a separate line. An operator who loses Grade B qualification loses ZN-A1 qualification at the same moment.

### Interventions

Interventions in ZN-A1 are the moments of highest risk. Each operator is qualified on the list of interventions in the media fill protocol, and each intervention is observed at least once a year. An intervention is done with the shortest possible movement, with the hands staying below the height of open vials, and it is recorded on the session log. An operator who is not qualified for an intervention does not perform it.

### Media fill participation

Every operator who works in ZN-A1 takes part in at least 2 media fills in each period of 12 months, in different sessions and in different roles where possible. An operator who has not taken part in 2 media fills within 12 months loses qualification for ZN-A1 until participation is completed. The media fill records name the operators present and the interventions each carried out, and the qualification coordinator checks them against the register every quarter.

## Conduct and access

### Illness and skin conditions

An operator who has an infectious illness, an open wound, a rash or any skin condition that could shed material must report it to the supervisor before gowning. After an illness with symptoms such as fever, cough or diarrhoea, the operator does not enter Grade B for 48 hours after the symptoms have resolved, and may return sooner only with clearance under the occupational health procedure OHP-0004. An operator with a skin condition is assessed and may be limited to Grade C or D duties.

### Behaviour rules

Cosmetics, jewellery, perfume and nail polish are not permitted in the classified areas, and hair, including beards, is fully covered by the hood. Personal items are left in RM-289. Food, drink and chewing gum are not permitted. Operators do not lean on equipment or walls, do not run, and do not reach across open product. Pens, paper and other loose items are not carried in; forms are read from the wall or passed through in a plastic sleeve that has been sanitised.

### Garment life

Sterile gowns are laundered and sterilised between uses, and each gown is retired after 30 laundering and sterilisation cycles, counted on the label of the garment. A gown that is torn, stained, shows a failed seam or cannot be traced to a cycle count is retired at once. The gowning supplier's certificate for each lot of garments states the cycles completed, and the supply chain team checks that the delivered garments match the order. The reuse limit is set by the supplier's validation and is not extended locally.

### Maintenance and engineering staff

Engineers and technicians who work in the classified rooms are qualified by the same programme, with the same sessions, sampling and intervals. Because they bring in tools, they also sanitise each tool before it crosses the airlock and carry tools in a closed sanitised tray. They are qualified for the grade in which they do most of their work, and a job in a higher grade is done only under escort by a qualified operator. Work in ZN-A1 by maintenance staff is planned in advance with QC, so that sampling is arranged around it.

### Visitors and contractors

Visitors to a Grade B room are limited to 2 at any one session, they must be escorted at all times by a qualified operator, and they gown under the observation of the qualification coordinator or a delegate. A visitor does not enter ZN-A1 and does not touch equipment. Contractors who need regular access follow the full qualification programme, and their qualification is recorded on the same register. The training arrangements and renewal interval for contractors are set out in the contractor training agreement CTA-0012.

### Trainees under supervision

A trainee who has completed the classroom part but not yet qualified may enter Grade C rooms under supervision. Entry to a Grade B room by a trainee is allowed only for the practical training and the qualification sessions, and a trainee never enters ZN-A1. A qualified operator may supervise no more than 1 trainee at a time. The supervisor is responsible for the trainee's behaviour, and a breach by a trainee is recorded against the supervising operator as well as the trainee.

### Access control

Entry to the classified rooms is controlled by badge readers linked to the qualification register. The system blocks the badge of an operator whose qualification has lapsed, whose media fill participation is incomplete or who is in a stand-down, and the block can be lifted only by the qualification coordinator after the register has been updated. Supervisors cannot override a block, and a person who enters without a valid badge is treated as an unqualified entrant and reported to QA. The list of blocked badges is reviewed at the start of each week.

## Lapse, emergencies and refresher training

### Lapse of qualification

An operator's qualification lapses if the operator has not entered Grade B for 60 days, whatever the reason. The operator then repeats a single observed and sampled session, and returns to Grade B work when it is successful. A lapse of more than 12 months, or a lapse combined with a failed session, requires the full initial qualification. The qualification coordinator informs the area supervisor of any lapse in advance, so that staff are not scheduled for duties they are no longer qualified to do.

### Emergencies

Anyone may leave the classified areas without gowning steps in an emergency such as a fire alarm, an injury or an evacuation, and nobody is to be delayed by the gowning rules in such a case. After the emergency, operators who left in a hurry re-enter only by completing the full gowning sequence again, and the area supervisor records who left, when and by which route. Product that was open when the emergency began is assessed by QA under SOP-QA-101, and the room is sampled at rest before work restarts.

### Exit and re-entry

An operator who leaves the Grade B area for a break, a meeting or any other reason removes the gown and puts on a clean garment on returning. Re-entry after a short exit for a matter of a few minutes, for example to fetch an item from the airlock, may be made in the same gown only if the gown has not been in contact with any non-sterile surface, and only if the exit was through the gowning area under the supervisor's control. The rules on re-entry are audited every year.

### Refresher training

Refresher training is given every 12 months to all qualified operators, and it is given sooner when the data suggest a problem: after a recovery in Grade A that was linked to gowning, after 3 observer findings in one quarter, or after an operator's return from leave of more than 60 days. The session repeats the sequence, shows the most common breaches recorded by the observers, and lasts at least 2 hours. Attendance is recorded on the qualification register, and an operator who has not attended within 14 months is treated as if the qualification had lapsed.

## Oversight and records

### Monitoring of performance

The qualification coordinator records, for each operator, the results of every session, the sampling results, the routine glove prints under SOP-QC-219 and the findings of the observers. A monthly review looks for operators whose counts are rising, for groups whose results are worse than others and for steps of the sequence that observers most often find broken. The results guide the choice of topics for refresher training and are reported to the QC microbiology manager.

### Cleaning of the gowning rooms

The gowning rooms are cleaned under SOP-MF-207, and the benches, the mirror and the garment cabinet are wiped at the start of each shift. The floor mats in RM-289 are replaced every 4 hours during busy periods. Environmental monitoring of the rooms follows SOP-QC-214, and a rise in recoveries from RM-291 is discussed with the qualification coordinator because it may signal a problem in how the room is used and not only in how it is cleaned.

### Records and the register

The qualification register lists each operator, the grades for which they are qualified, the date of the last successful session, the next due date and the dates of media fill participation. It is reviewed every month and is available to supervisors at all times. Records of sessions, sampling results, retraining and stand-downs are retained as set in the records retention policy POL-0004.

### Breaches of aseptic behaviour

A breach of the behaviour rules is reported to the area supervisor when it is seen. A breach in ZN-A1 during filling, such as a hand passing over open vials or a glove touching a non-sterile surface, is reported to QA in the same shift, because it may affect product. QA decides whether a deviation is needed under SOP-QA-101, and the operator is withdrawn from ZN-A1 pending an observed session. A pattern of breaches by one operator triggers retraining, and the pattern is noted in the qualification register.

### Shifts and continuity

The qualification programme has to serve night and weekend shifts as well as days. Sessions for these shifts are scheduled at the start of the shift, with an observer and an analyst on call, and a session missed because of shift work is rebooked within 7 days. Where a whole shift team is due for requalification in the same month, the qualification coordinator spreads the sessions over the 4 weeks before the due date so that the area never has too many operators away at once.

### Internal checks of the register

Each quarter, the QA reviewer picks 10 operators at random and compares the register with what actually happened: the date of the last session, the sampling results, the retraining records and the media fill participation. A difference is treated as a documentation finding, and a difference that means an operator worked without valid qualification is treated as a deviation under SOP-QA-101. The results of the checks are reported to the QC microbiology manager together with the annual programme review.

### Programme review

The programme is reviewed once a year by the QA reviewer, the QC microbiology manager and the qualification coordinator. The review looks at the failure rate for the year, the causes of failures, the time between qualification and any recovery in Grade A, the observer findings and the training feedback. Changes to the acceptance criteria, sampling sites or intervals go through change control under SOP-QA-118 before they take effect.
