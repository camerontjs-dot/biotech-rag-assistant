# Calibration Programme and Interval Management

## Purpose and applicability

Every instrument whose reading supports a decision about product, equipment or the environment has to be shown to read correctly, and to go on doing so between checks. This procedure defines how instruments are classified, how often they are calibrated, what counts as acceptable, and what has to happen when an instrument is found to have drifted. It applies to instruments owned by Engineering and Facilities, Quality Control and Manufacturing Operations across the site, including the balances (EQ-0801, EQ-0802 and EQ-0806), pH meters (EQ-0815 and EQ-0816), pressure gauges, pipettes, timers, thermometers, flow meters and conductivity meters listed below.

## Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Calibration technician | Performs calibrations, records as-found and as-left readings on FRM-EN-201, updates status labels |
| Instrument owner | Keeps the instrument available for calibration, reports damage, decides limited-use restrictions with QA |
| Engineering supervisor | Maintains the calibration schedule and reviews overdue and out-of-tolerance lists each week |
| QA reviewer | Approves interval extensions and out-of-tolerance impact assessments that touch release results |

## Instrument classes

Every instrument is assigned one class when it is entered into the schedule, and the class is reviewed when its use changes.

| Class | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Class 1 | The reading feeds a release decision, a limit test or a safety function |
| Class 2 | The reading supports process control but does not decide release |
| Class 3 | Indication only; no reading is recorded or used as evidence |

Class 3 instruments are not entered into the calibration schedule. They carry a label stating that they are for indication only, and an instrument that begins to be used for a record is reclassified before its next use.

## Calibration intervals

| Instrument | Interval |
| --- | --- |
| Analytical balances | 6 months |
| Top-loading balances | 6 months |
| pH meters | 3 months |
| Differential pressure gauges | 6 months |
| Pipettes | 6 months |
| Flow meters | 6 months |
| Conductivity meters | 6 months |
| Reference thermometer | 12 months |
| Laboratory timers | 12 months |
| Cold room and stability chamber probes | 12 months |
| Check-weight set, external recertification | 24 months |

The intervals run from the date the calibration is completed. Where an instrument is used only in campaigns, it is calibrated before the campaign starts rather than on a fixed date, provided the interval would otherwise fall inside the campaign.

## Timing

A calibration may be carried out up to 10 days before the due date without changing the schedule; the next due date is then counted from the original date. There is no late window. An instrument that reaches its due date without a completed calibration is out of service from that moment, and the red label is applied by the calibration technician or by the instrument owner without waiting for a reminder.

## Tolerances

The balance and pH meter tolerances are set out in CAL-0507. For the other instruments the acceptance criteria are as follows.

- Differential pressure gauge EQ-0831: plus or minus 2 Pa at a reading of 15 Pa.
- Reference thermometer EQ-0825 and its probes: plus or minus 0.3 degrees C against the certified standard.
- Laboratory timer EQ-0840: plus or minus 1 second per hour against the reference clock.
- Conductivity meter EQ-0845: plus or minus 0.05 uS/cm when reading the 1.30 uS/cm standard MAT-2610.
- Flow meter EQ-0850: the acceptance tolerance is taken from the manufacturer datasheet MDS-0850, and the value is entered on FRM-EN-201 at each calibration.

An instrument found outside tolerance at the as-found reading is adjusted, and the as-left reading must be inside tolerance before the instrument is released. Both readings are always recorded, even when the as-found reading is acceptable.

## Status labels

Each instrument carries a label showing the date of calibration, the due date and the technician's initials. A green label means in calibration. A yellow label means limited use, with the restriction written on the label, for example a reduced range. A red label means out of service. A label is never moved from one instrument to another, and a red-labelled instrument must not be used for any record. The instrument owner removes a red-labelled instrument from the work area or seals it so that it cannot be picked up by mistake.

## Out-of-tolerance findings

When the as-found reading is outside tolerance, an impact assessment is opened on FRM-EN-205 within 1 working day of the finding, and it is completed within 10 working days. The look-back covers every use of the instrument since its last in-tolerance calibration. The assessment lists the affected batches, tests and records, states whether the result could have changed a decision, and proposes retesting where it could. QA approves the assessment on FRM-EN-205 whenever a release result is affected. Where product impact is possible, a deviation is raised under SOP-QA-101 in parallel and the two records refer to each other.

## Interval extension

An interval for a Class 2 instrument may be lengthened by up to 25 percent when its last 3 calibrations were all within tolerance and the change is approved by QA on FRM-EN-210. Class 1 instruments are never extended. The extension limit for Class 3 instruments that have been reclassified for a limited purpose is taken from the interval risk assessment RAS-0044. An instrument whose extension request is refused is calibrated on the original schedule.

## Standards and traceability

Reference standards are certified by an accredited external laboratory, and the probes of the cold room CR-1 and the freezer FZ-1 (EQ-0910, EQ-0911 and EQ-0915) are calibrated against the reference thermometer, and working standards are verified against the reference standards every 12 months. The check-weight set EQ-0820 is recertified externally on the interval shown above. The certificates are filed with the instrument records and are checked for the range and uncertainty that the calibrated instruments need. A standard that is dropped, corroded or found outside its certificate is withdrawn until it has been recertified.

## Vendor and on-site service

Where a calibration is carried out by an outside vendor, the certificate must show as-found and as-left readings and the standards used. The interval for the vendor's on-site service visit to the differential pressure gauges is set in the calibration service contract CSA-0021. For the balances the recalibration interval and tolerance of the manufacturer's internal adjustment weight follow the balance qualification report QRP-0801, and the technician does not adjust the internal weight during a routine calibration.

## Records

Calibration records are retained for the period set in POL-0004. Paper bench logbooks kept under the earlier practice in SOP-EN-044 are archived with the instrument file, and new entries are made on FRM-EN-201 and, for balances, FRM-QC-141. Portable air sampler flow meters used for the monitoring programme described in SOP-QC-214 are calibrated on the interval in the table above.
