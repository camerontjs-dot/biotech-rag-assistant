# Supplier Qualification and Audit Planning

## Purpose

The quality of a drug product starts with the quality of what goes into it, and that depends on the suppliers who make and deliver those materials. This procedure describes how suppliers of starting materials, excipients, primary packaging and services that touch product are assessed before they are approved, how they are audited afterwards, and how their performance is watched. Sampling and release of what they deliver is covered by SOP-WH-071.

## Supplier categories

Every supplier is placed in one category, according to what it supplies and what would happen if its material failed.

| Category | Typical supply | On-site audit | Audit length |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Critical | Sterile components, primary packaging, excipients that enter the product directly | Every 24 months | 2 days |
| Major | Secondary packaging, buffer salts, cleaning agents | Every 36 months | 1 day |
| Minor | Labels, cartons, general consumables | Desktop assessment only, every 60 months | Not applicable |

The category is proposed by the supply chain quality lead and approved by QA. A supplier that sells several kinds of material takes the category of the most critical one. A category is reviewed when the material changes or the supplier's performance changes.

## Steps to qualification

1. The buyer nominates the supplier and states the material, its intended use and the expected annual volume.
2. The supplier receives the questionnaire FRM-WH-501 and returns it within 30 days of issue.
3. The supply chain quality lead carries out a desktop assessment of the answers, the certificates and the supplier's inspection history.
4. For Critical and Major suppliers, an on-site audit is planned and carried out.
5. Trial lots are ordered, sampled and tested under SOP-WH-071.
6. QA approves the supplier and enters it on the approved supplier list.

A supplier that does not return the questionnaire in time is reminded once, and the nomination lapses 30 days after the reminder if there is still no answer.

## The questionnaire and the desktop assessment

FRM-WH-501 asks about the supplier's quality system, sites, sub-suppliers, change control, complaint handling, batch traceability and regulatory history. The reviewer looks for evidence, not assertions: certificates that are current, procedures that are named, and an inspection history that is disclosed and explained. Where an answer is vague, a follow-up question is sent, and the reviewer records the exchange. The desktop assessment takes no more than 10 days from the return of the questionnaire and ends in a recommendation on whether to audit, to accept on paper, or to decline.

## On-site audit

The audit dates are proposed 60 days ahead and fixed with at least 30 days of notice to the supplier. It follows an agenda based on the material and the supplier's history, and it is carried out by a qualified auditor and, for Critical suppliers, a second person with technical knowledge of the material. A Critical supplier is audited for 2 days, and a Major supplier for 1 day. The audit covers the quality system, the manufacturing area, storage, testing and records, and it ends with a closing meeting at which the findings are read out.

## Audit report and supplier response

The auditor issues the report on FRM-WH-505 within 20 working days of the end of the audit. QA reviews each report within 5 days of receiving it, and the opening meeting of an audit lasts about 45 minutes. A Minor supplier that has had no rejected lot for 10 years is reviewed by desktop assessment only. The supplier answers each finding with a proposed action and a date within 15 working days of receiving the report. The supply chain quality lead reviews the answers and either accepts them or asks for more. For Critical findings, the closure is verified within 90 days, by documentary evidence or by a further visit. Findings that are not closed within their agreed dates are escalated to QA, and the supplier's status is reviewed.

## Trial lots

Before full qualification, a supplier delivers trial lots. Three consecutive lots must comply with the specification and with all receipt checks before the supplier is fully qualified. A failure of any trial lot restarts the count from zero, and it is investigated with the supplier. Trial lots are used in production only where the material specification and the risk assessment allow it, and otherwise they are tested and held back.

## The approved supplier list

QA keeps the approved supplier list, which shows each supplier, its category, the materials for which it is approved, its status and the date of its last audit. The list is reviewed every 12 months, and an audit that is more than 3 months past its due date is escalated to the QA Head. Only suppliers on the list may be ordered from. A supplier that is not on the list must not ship product to the site, and an order to such a supplier is blocked in the purchasing system. Supplier identifiers take the form SUP-nnnn.

## Performance monitoring

The supply chain quality lead reports supplier performance every quarter, covering rejected lots, late deliveries, complaints and open audit findings. A supplier with 2 rejected lots in 12 months has its status changed to conditional, which means each lot is tested in full and CoA reliance is withdrawn. A conditional supplier is reassessed after 6 months without a rejected lot. A supplier with 3 rejected lots in 12 months is suspended, and no order is placed until a corrective action has been accepted and a new trial lot has passed. Each rejection is handled as a deviation under SOP-QA-101, and where a systemic cause is suspected, a CAPA is raised under SOP-QA-172.

## Changes at the supplier

A supplier must tell the site of any change to the material, the process, the site of manufacture or the sub-suppliers, and the site assesses the change under SOP-QA-118 using the matrix in SPC-2245. A change of manufacturing site by a supplier requires requalification, including a fresh assessment and trial lots, before material from the new site is used. A supplier that changes without notice is reviewed for suspension.

## Quality agreements

Critical and Major suppliers sign a quality agreement that states the responsibilities for testing, change notification, deviation handling and audit access. The audit frequency for suppliers of reference standards is agreed in the quality agreement QAG-0044, since the material is supplied in small quantities and often by a single source, and this arrangement overrides the frequencies in the table above for those suppliers only. A typical delivery is a few vials of 100 mg each, and the agreement lists the certificate that must accompany every lot.

## Retired questionnaire practice

The paper questionnaire that used to be posted to suppliers under SOP-WH-019 has been replaced by FRM-WH-501. Suppliers that qualified under the earlier practice were reassessed on the new form at their next audit, and their earlier answers are kept in the supplier file.

## Records

The supplier file holds the questionnaire, the assessments, the audit reports, the quality agreement, the trial lot results and the performance reports. Files are retained as set in POL-0004 and remain accessible while the supplier is on the list.
