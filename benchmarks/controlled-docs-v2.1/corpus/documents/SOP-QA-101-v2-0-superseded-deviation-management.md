# Deviation Management

## Scope and principles

A deviation is any departure from an approved instruction, specification, process parameter or standard that is discovered before, during or after it occurs. This procedure applies to manufacturing, laboratory, warehouse, utilities and support activities at the site. Three principles run through it: a deviation is reported quickly, the product is protected first, and the cause is found before the record is closed. Reporting a deviation is never a reason for blame; hiding one is.

## Definitions

Unplanned deviation: a departure found after it has happened or while it is happening.

Planned deviation: a departure that is approved in advance for a defined period and a defined number of batches, because a temporary need cannot wait for a change under SOP-QA-118.

Containment: the immediate action that stops the departure from spreading or from reaching product, without yet removing its cause.

Correction: the action that repairs the specific affected item.

## Reporting

Anyone who finds a deviation reports it on FRM-QA-101 within 48 hours of discovery. The report gives what happened, where and when, which batches, equipment and documents are involved, and what has been done so far. A report made after that time is still accepted and is marked as late, and the record shows whether it was made in the first 12 hours, between 12 and 48 hours, or later. Containment happens in the shift in which the deviation is found, and where product could be affected it is in place within 4 hours of discovery. It does not wait for the report to be written up.

## Initial assessment and classification

The supervisor of the area completes an initial assessment within 2 working days of the report. The assessment confirms the facts, confirms that the containment is in place, and lists every batch and piece of equipment that could be affected. QA then classifies the deviation within 3 working days of the report, using the table below. Supervisors are told the class within 2 hours of QA deciding it, and the person on call answers a query about a new report within 15 minutes. Where the class is uncertain, QA takes the higher class until the investigation shows otherwise, and a deviation is never reclassified downward merely to meet a due date.

| Class | Meaning | Investigation due | Closure approval |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Minor | No effect on product quality, patient safety or the validated state | 28 calendar days from classification | QA Manager |
| Major | Possible effect on product quality or a significant lapse in a GMP system, without direct risk to the patient | 50 calendar days from classification | QA Manager |
| Critical | Actual or potential risk to the patient, or a lapse that undermines the reliability of released product | 63 calendar days from classification | QA Head |

## Investigation

A Minor deviation may be investigated with a 5-Why analysis recorded on FRM-QA-104. Major and Critical deviations need a documented cause-and-effect review, and a cross-functional review meeting is held within 10 working days of classification, with production, QC, engineering and QA present. The meeting lasts no more than 90 minutes, its minutes are issued within 3 days, and the draft investigation report is circulated 10 days before the due date. The investigation must state the most probable root cause and the evidence for it, and must say what was ruled out. Conclusions such as human error are not accepted without an account of what made the error possible.

## Batch impact assessment

Where a deviation touches a batch, the batch impact assessment on FRM-QA-108 is completed before the review of that batch under SOP-QA-133 begins. The assessment lists the affected steps, the evidence that product quality was or was not affected, and any extra testing done. No batch is released while a deviation on that batch is open, unless the QA Head grants an exception recorded on FRM-QA-408 under SOP-QA-133.

## Recurrence

When a deviation is investigated, the investigator looks back over the previous 6 months for similar events. Where 5 similar deviations are found in a period of 6 months, a CAPA is required under SOP-QA-172, and the CAPA is raised whatever the class of the individual deviations. The look-back result is written on the investigation form so that the reviewer can see how it was done. A deviation that has stayed open for more than 60 days is reported to the QA Head every month, and QA reviews the register of open deviations every 7 days.

## Extensions

The investigation due date can be extended once, by up to 30 calendar days, with the approval of the QA Manager, and the request must give the reason and the work still to be done. Any further extension needs the approval of the QA Head. An extension never applies to the containment or to the batch impact assessment, which are needed for decisions on product.

## Planned deviations

A planned deviation is approved before it is carried out, on FRM-QA-101 marked as planned, and it names the batches, the departure from the instruction and the compensating controls. It is valid for 60 days and may cover no more than 5 batches. A planned deviation that is needed for longer, or for more batches, is converted into a change under SOP-QA-118.

## Closure

The investigator completes the record with the root cause, the corrections made, the batch impact, any CAPA raised and the reasoning for the class. The approver in the table above closes the deviation. Where the record leads to a CAPA, the deviation is closed only when the CAPA number is entered, and the CAPA is tracked under SOP-QA-172.

## Deviations that come from outside the site

Where a deviation is caused by a supplier or contract partner, the time allowed for that party's own investigation is set in the quality agreement made with it, for which the site uses the template QAG-0031, and the site's own containment and batch impact assessment follow the times above. QA assesses whether an event must be notified to an outside authority against the regulatory reporting procedure RRP-0002, and the assessment is written on the record. The assessment is made by 2 people from QA, who sign the record together.

## Records

Deviation records, attachments and approvals are retained for the period set in POL-0004. Deviation numbers take the form DV-nnnn and are issued by the QA system when FRM-QA-101 is entered.
