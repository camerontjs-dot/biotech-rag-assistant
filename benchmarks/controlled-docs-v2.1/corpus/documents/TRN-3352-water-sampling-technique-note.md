# Water Sampling Technique Note

## About this note

A water sample is only as good as the way it was taken. This note is for laboratory staff and utilities technicians who take or receive samples from the purified water loop LP-01 and the water for injection loop LP-02. It gives the practical steps and the numbers you meet most often. The controlling documents are SOP-EN-152 for sampling and SPC-2252 for the levels; if anything here seems to conflict with them, ask before you sample.

## Before you go to the point

Collect sterile 100 mL bottles (MAT-5015), labels, a marker, 70 percent isopropyl alcohol and a clean transport box. Check the sampling list for your points and note which are return points, since PT-12 and PT-28 are sampled every day. Put on clean gloves and a mask before you touch the bottles.

## At the outlet

1. Spray the outside of the outlet with 70 percent isopropyl alcohol and wait 30 seconds.
2. Open the valve fully and let the water run to drain for 1 minute.
3. Fill the bottle to the mark, holding it clear of the outlet.
4. Close the bottle at once and label it with the point number, the time and your initials.
5. Close the valve, and write the time on the sampling record.

Do not adjust the flow to a trickle to make filling easier. A trickle can draw water back from the fittings, and the sample will not represent the loop.

## Getting the sample to the laboratory

Samples must be in the laboratory within 2 hours of collection. If you know you will be delayed, tell the laboratory before you leave the point. Keep the box upright, out of the sun and away from warm equipment. A sample that arrives late is discarded and the point is sampled again; do not talk yourself into testing an old sample because the point is inconvenient to reach.

## Plating and incubation

Membrane filtration is used for WFI and pour plates for PW, both on R2A agar (MAT-5010). Plates are incubated at 20 to 25 degrees C and read after 3 days. Write the incubation start on the plate at the time of plating, and check the incubator temperature at the same time.

## What the numbers mean

For purified water, an in-line conductivity of 1.1 uS/cm is an alert and 1.4 uS/cm is an action. For WFI, a conductivity of 0.9 uS/cm is an alert and 1.2 uS/cm is an action. When a point is at an alert level, tell the utilities engineer and expect to take another sample within 48 hours. When a point is at an action level, the outlet is tagged, QA is told, and nobody uses that water until the point has been cleared.

## Sanitization of the loops

You will sometimes be asked to take samples after a sanitization. The WFI loop is sanitised with hot water at 80 degrees C for 60 minutes, and the PW loop on a longer schedule. After the flush the first sample is taken on the next day, and sampling continues daily until the loop is released. Never take a sample from a loop that has not been flushed, even if you are told the result would be interesting.

## Common errors

- Not flushing the outlet long enough, so the sample shows the state of the valve and not of the loop.
- Filling the bottle above the mark and having to pour some away.
- Touching the inside of the cap.
- Labelling the bottle after it is in the box instead of at the point.
- Leaving samples on the bench while finishing other work.
