# Deviation Triage for Floor Supervisors

## Purpose of this note

Supervisors are the first people to hear about a departure from the instructions, and what they do in the first hour shapes the whole record. This note walks through the first steps in plain terms. It sits beside SOP-QA-101, which controls; if you are unsure how a rule applies, ask your QA contact before you file.

## The first hour

1. Stop the work that is affected, or make it safe to continue.
2. Protect product: hold anything that could be involved, and label it.
3. Write down what you can see while it is fresh: time, place, batch, equipment, who was there.
4. Tell your manager and the QA contact for the area.

Do not wait until you have the cause. You do not need to know why it happened to report that it happened.

## Reporting

Report on FRM-QA-101 within 48 hours of finding the deviation. That is a limit, not a target; most reports are in the same shift. If you are not sure whether something is a deviation, report it anyway and let QA decide, since a wrongly reported item costs a few minutes and a missed one can cost a batch.

## What you do in the two days after

You complete the initial assessment within 2 working days of the report. It has three parts: confirm the facts you first wrote down, confirm the containment is holding, and list every batch and piece of equipment that might be touched. Send the list to QA; they are waiting for it to classify the deviation.

## How QA classifies

QA classifies the deviation within 3 working days of the report as Minor, Major or Critical. As a rule of thumb, Minor means no effect on quality, Major means a possible effect or a real lapse in a system, and Critical means a risk to the patient. You will be asked for the investigation dates once the class is set: 28 days for a Minor deviation, 50 days for a Major one and 63 days for a Critical one. If you need more time, ask early. One extension of up to 30 days is available with the QA Manager's approval.

## Getting the investigation started

For a Minor deviation a 5-Why analysis on FRM-QA-104 is enough. For a Major or Critical deviation there is a cross-functional meeting within 10 working days of classification. Bring the timeline, the batch list and any records from the equipment, and bring the operators who saw it happen. Ask what made the mistake possible, not only who made it.

## Looking back

Before you sign off the investigation, search the system for similar events in the previous 6 months. If you find 5 or more, tell QA at once, because a CAPA becomes mandatory.

## Planned deviations

If you know in advance that a batch has to run outside the instruction, do not wait for it to become a deviation. Ask for a planned deviation. It is valid for 60 days and can cover up to 5 batches, and it has to name the compensating controls.

## Things that catch people out

- Releasing held product because the batch record looks clean.
- Describing the deviation as "operator error" and stopping there.
- Sending the batch list after QA has already classified the event.
- Letting the investigation date pass without asking for an extension.
- Forgetting to write the CAPA number on the record before closure.
