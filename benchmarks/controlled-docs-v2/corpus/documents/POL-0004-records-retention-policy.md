# Records Retention Policy

## Purpose

Records are the evidence that work was done as required, and the site keeps them for as long as they may be needed to answer a question about a product, a patient, an instrument or a decision. This policy sets the minimum period for which each kind of record is kept, how records are stored and protected during that time, and how they are destroyed at the end of it. It applies to paper and electronic records in every department.

## Principles

Records are kept in a form that stays legible and retrievable for the whole retention period. A record is kept in its original form unless a certified copy has replaced it under an approved process. No record is destroyed before its period has ended, and no record is kept beyond its period without a reason, since records kept without need create cost and risk. The owner of a record type is responsible for applying this policy to it.

## Retention periods

The periods below are minimums. Where a customer agreement or a legal requirement asks for a longer period, the longer period applies.

| Record | Retention period |
| --- | --- |
| Master batch records | Lifetime of the product plus 6 years |
| Executed batch records | 12 years after release |
| Deviation and CAPA records | 8 years after closure |
| Complaint records | 10 years after closure |
| Training records | 6 years after the employee leaves |
| Calibration records | 5 years after the instrument is retired |
| Environmental monitoring raw data | 7 years |
| Equipment logbooks | 3 years after the last entry |
| Superseded controlled documents | 10 years |

## Records under legal hold

A record that is or may become relevant to an inspection, a dispute or an investigation is placed under legal hold by the QA Head and is kept until the QA Head releases it in writing, even if its normal period has ended. The hold is recorded in the register with its date and reason, and the record is marked so that routine destruction skips it.

## Electronic records and archive services

The retention of electronic audit trails for GMP systems follows the data governance procedure DGP-0003, which sets the period per system. Records held off site are retrieved within the time agreed in the archive service agreement ASA-0005, and the retrieval of records for clinical supplies follows the terms of the sponsor agreement for the study concerned. Electronic records are backed up every day, and a backup is tested by restoring a sample of records every 3 months.

## Storage of paper records

Paper records are stored in the archive room in boxes labelled with the record type, the period covered and the destruction date. The room is kept between 15 and 25 degrees C and below 60 percent RH, with fire-rated cabinets for records of the highest importance, rated for at least 60 minutes. Access is limited to named archive staff, and every removal and return is logged. Records taken out of the archive are returned within 5 working days, or the log shows where they are.

## Retrieval

Records held on site are retrieved within 1 working day of a request, and an urgent request from QA is answered on the same day. The requester records what was taken and why. A record that cannot be found is reported to the QA Head, and the search that was made is recorded.

## Destruction

At the end of its period a record is reviewed for legal hold and for any open question that needs it. If none is found, destruction is approved by the record owner and QA, carried out by shredding or secure deletion under a witnessed record signed by 2 people, and the destruction certificate is kept for 10 years. Destruction is done in a batch once each quarter, so that the review is systematic and not ad hoc.

## Responsibilities

The QA Head owns this policy and reviews it every 36 months, and the register of record types is checked against the departments' practice once every 12 months. Department heads apply it to the record types of their areas, and the document controller keeps the register of record types, periods and hold notices. Any exception, for example a shorter period for a superseded record that has no further use, needs written approval from the QA Head.
