# Corrective and Preventive Action Management

## Why this procedure exists

A deviation that is contained but never fixed will come back. This procedure governs the corrective and preventive action (CAPA) system, which turns the lessons from deviations, audits, complaints and trends into changes that last. It says who may open a CAPA, how it is classified, how long each stage may take, and what evidence is needed before it is closed. It applies to every department at the site.

## Sources of a CAPA

A CAPA may be opened from any of the following.

- A deviation whose investigation found a systemic cause, raised under SOP-QA-101.
- A product complaint whose investigation found a manufacturing or design cause, handled under SOP-QA-146.
- An audit or inspection observation.
- A trend signal from monitoring, stability or supplier data.
- A management review decision.

Opening a CAPA is a decision, not a reflex. Where a deviation has a single obvious and fully contained cause, the correction may be closed inside the deviation record and no CAPA is opened.

## Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| QA CAPA coordinator | Logs requests, assigns the risk class, tracks due dates, prepares the board agenda |
| Action owner | Writes the plan, carries out the actions, supplies closure evidence |
| Action owner's line manager | Receives overdue reminders and clears obstacles |
| Effectiveness check owner | Designs and carries out the check; is never the action owner |
| CAPA review board | Approves second extensions, reviews overdue actions and recurring causes |
| QA Manager and QA Head | Approve closure by class |

## Opening a CAPA

The requester completes FRM-QA-201 with the source record, a description of the problem and the proposed scope. The request is logged within 5 working days of the decision to open it, and the coordinator returns a CAPA number within 1 day. The QA CAPA coordinator gives the CAPA a number in the form CP-nnnn and assigns the risk class from the table below, using the scoring method in the risk management procedure RMP-0009, which also fixes the score at which a CAPA counts as Critical.

## Risk classes and timelines

| Class | Corrective action due | Effectiveness check | Closure approval |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Critical | 60 calendar days from plan approval | 120 days after the last action closes | QA Head |
| Major | 120 calendar days from plan approval | 60 days after the last action closes | QA Manager |
| Minor | 150 calendar days from plan approval | None, unless the same issue recurs within 12 months | QA reviewer |

For a Critical CAPA, containment starts at once and is in place by the end of the shift in which the CAPA is opened; it does not wait for the plan.

## Investigation and root cause

The action owner establishes the root cause before a plan can be approved. A root cause is a condition that, had it been absent, would have prevented the event, and that can itself be changed. "Operator error" is not accepted as a root cause on its own; the investigation continues to ask why the error was possible, for example an ambiguous instruction, a poorly placed control or a training gap. Evidence for the cause is attached to the record, and where the cause cannot be confirmed the plan states what was ruled out and what will be watched.

## The CAPA plan

The plan is written on FRM-QA-204 and submitted within 15 working days of opening for Critical and Major CAPAs, and within 20 working days for Minor ones. It lists each action with a named owner and a due date, separates corrections and containment from corrective and preventive actions, states the effectiveness measure, and identifies any action that needs a change under SOP-QA-118 or a revision of a document under SOP-QA-105. A plan whose actions all depend on retraining is challenged by the QA CAPA coordinator, because retraining alone rarely removes the cause.

## Extensions

An action owner who cannot meet a due date requests an extension on FRM-QA-207. The request must be lodged at least 5 working days before the due date and must say what has been done, why the date will be missed and what the new date is. The first extension, of up to 45 calendar days, is approved by the QA Manager. A second extension of up to 45 calendar days is approved by the CAPA review board, and a third by the QA Head. A fourth extension is not permitted; if the work is still not finished, the CAPA is closed with its status stated and a new CAPA is opened for the remaining work.

## Effectiveness checks

An effectiveness check tests whether the cause has really gone. It is designed at the planning stage, before the actions are finished, and follows the guidance of the CAPA review board. The check owner must not be the action owner, because the person who made the change is the least well placed to judge it. Results are reported on FRM-QA-210. A Minor CAPA has no routine check, but a repeat of the same issue within 12 months triggers one.

## Closure

The action owner assembles the evidence for every action and completes the closure checklist FRM-QA-214. The approver named in the risk class table reviews the evidence and either closes the CAPA or returns it with a list of gaps. A CAPA must not be closed on the plan alone: there has to be evidence that each action was carried out, and for Critical and Major CAPAs, that the effectiveness check met its criterion.

## Overdue actions and escalation

An action that is one working day past its due date without an approved extension prompts a reminder to the owner's line manager. An action that is 20 working days past its due date goes on the agenda of the CAPA review board, and the owner attends to explain the delay.

## The CAPA review board

The board meets every 2 weeks, and a meeting needs at least 3 members to go ahead. It reviews new Critical and Major CAPAs, all overdue actions, second-extension requests and any root cause that has now produced more than one CAPA. Where 4 CAPAs share the same root cause within 12 months, the QA Head reviews the trend and decides whether the cause has been treated at the right level.

## Measures

Minutes of each board meeting are issued within 3 days. Every quarter QA samples 10 percent of the CAPAs closed in that period and checks the quality of their evidence. The QA CAPA coordinator reports the proportion of CAPAs closed by their due date every quarter. The target is 85 percent. The report also shows the number open by class, the number of extensions granted and the number of CAPAs reopened.

## Supplier-originated actions

Where a CAPA depends on a supplier, the time allowed for the supplier's part of the work is fixed in the quality agreement with that supplier, as set out in the agreement template QAG-0031, and the site's own actions still keep the due dates above. The CAPA is not held open indefinitely while the supplier responds; the site records what it has done and escalates through the supplier manager. The supplier manager contacts the supplier every 10 days about an open supplier action and records each contact on the CAPA.

## Keeping the register current

The QA CAPA coordinator reviews the register every 7 days and updates the status of each open CAPA. Board papers are circulated 3 days before each meeting so that owners can prepare. A CAPA with no update for 30 days is queried with its owner, and an owner who has changed role hands the actions over in writing within 2 days of the change. Evidence files are uploaded within 2 days of an action closing, and owners of Critical CAPAs give a progress update every 14 days and owners of Major and Minor CAPAs every 30 days, and the register shows the last 12 months of closed CAPAs by default so that recent history is easy to find. Owners receive a reminder 14 days before each due date. During the first 72 hours of a Critical CAPA the coordinator checks progress every 12 hours, and questions from owners are answered within 4 hours. An action is shown amber when it is within 30 days of its due date with no recorded progress, and red when it is within 7 days. At every meeting the board reviews the trend in overdue actions over the last 6 months.
