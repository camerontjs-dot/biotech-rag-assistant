# Action Owner Briefing

## If you have just been named an action owner

You now own one or more actions in a CAPA, and this briefing is the short version of what you need to know. The controlling procedure is SOP-QA-172, and the guidance on how to design an effectiveness check is in TCN-4102. If you are unsure, ask the QA CAPA coordinator before a date passes, not after.

## What you are on the hook for

You write the plan, you carry out the actions, and you gather the evidence that they were done. You do not close the CAPA and you do not judge whether it worked; other people do those two things on purpose.

## Dates that matter

- The plan on FRM-QA-204 is due within 10 working days of the CAPA being opened if it is Critical or Major, and within 15 working days if it is Minor.
- Your corrective actions are due 45 calendar days after plan approval for a Critical CAPA, 90 for a Major CAPA and 120 for a Minor one.
- The effectiveness check follows the last action: 180 days later for Critical, 90 days later for Major, and none for Minor unless the same problem comes back within a year.

Put the due dates in your own calendar with a reminder 14 days ahead. The system reminds your line manager, not you, when a date is missed.

## If you cannot make a date

Ask for an extension on FRM-QA-207. The request must reach the QA Manager at least 5 working days before the due date, and it has to say what is done, what is left and the date you can meet. You can have one extension of up to 30 calendar days from the QA Manager. A second one has to go to the CAPA review board, and there is no third: if it is still open after that, the CAPA is closed with its status stated and the rest of the work becomes a new CAPA.

## Containment for a Critical CAPA

Do not wait for the plan. Containment has to be in place by the end of the shift in which the CAPA was opened, and you should write down what you did and the time you did it.

## Before the due date

Send a draft of your plan to the coordinator 3 days before it is due so that it can be read in time, and send your evidence pack to QA at least 5 days before the due date, so that any gap can be closed while the action is still on time. The coordinator reads every pack within 2 days of receiving it.

## What good evidence looks like

Evidence is something a stranger could pick up in a year and understand. A signed training sheet shows that people attended; it does not show that the work changed. A revised procedure with its effective date, a photograph of a fitted guard, an extract from the system showing the new setting, or a screenshot of the new alert are all evidence. Attach each item to the action it supports and label it, then complete the closure checklist FRM-QA-214 with the QA CAPA coordinator.

## Things you must not do

- Do not close your own effectiveness check. Someone else runs it.
- Do not change the measure after the actions are done.
- Do not treat operator error as the cause without asking why the error was possible.
- Do not let a date pass without either finishing or requesting an extension.

## Your first 30 days

In the first 2 days read the CAPA record and the source deviation or complaint. By the end of the first week, meet the coordinator and agree who else must contribute. Within 10 working days for a Critical or Major CAPA the plan is in, so book 2 half-days for the investigation and 1 for writing. Keep a folder for evidence from the start and add to it at least once every 7 days, because reconstructing a month of work from memory is where gaps appear. An action that needs a purchase order should be raised at least 6 weeks ahead, and the plan should be with the coordinator 1 week before the approval meeting.

## Who to call

The QA CAPA coordinator owns the register and can tell you the status of any CAPA. Your line manager is copied on overdue reminders and can help clear a blockage. The board meets every 2 weeks, and an action that is 10 working days late goes on its agenda whether or not you are ready.
