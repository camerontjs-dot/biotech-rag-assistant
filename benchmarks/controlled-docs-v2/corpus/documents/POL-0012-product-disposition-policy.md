# Product Disposition Policy

## Policy statement

The site releases product only when the evidence shows that it was made, tested and stored as required and that it meets its specification and its shelf life commitments. Every batch has exactly one disposition at any time, and the disposition is decided by a named person with the authority to make it. The QA Head names no more than 4 designates at once. Commercial pressure, customer deadlines and the cost of scrapping product are never grounds for a disposition that the evidence does not support.

## Dispositions

There are three dispositions.

- Released: the batch may be supplied.
- Rejected: the batch must not be supplied and is to be destroyed.
- Held: the batch is not available for supply while a question is open.

Conditional release is not permitted. A batch may not be released on the understanding that a missing test, a missing record or an open investigation will be completed later. Where product is needed before all the evidence is in, the only route is the exception process of SOP-QA-133, which permits disposition with a single defined open item and only for a deviation that is not Critical.

## Who decides

Release is signed by the QA Head or by a designate named in writing by the QA Head. A designate must have at least 3 years of experience in quality assurance at the site and has to be trained on this policy and on SOP-QA-133 before the first signature. The list of designates is reviewed every 12 months. A designate must not release a batch that they reviewed themselves, since the release decision has to be independent of the review that supports it.

## Holds

A quality event on a batch, such as a deviation, a failed test, a complaint or an equipment failure, leads to a hold. The hold decision is made within 24 hours of the event becoming known, and the batch is physically segregated and labelled, and it is re-inspected every 14 days to confirm that its labels and its location are unchanged. Held batches are listed at the weekly quality meeting, and a batch that has been on hold for more than 90 days is escalated to the QA Head with a written plan for its disposition. A hold is lifted only by the person who could release the batch, and the reason is entered on the record within 1 day.

## Remaining shelf life at release

A batch is released for supply only if at least 70 percent of its labelled shelf life remains on the day of release. The requirement for lots shipped for export is set by the customer agreement CAG-0018 for the market concerned, and where an agreement gives a different remaining shelf life, the agreement applies and the difference is stated on the certificate.

## Rejection and destruction

A rejected batch is destroyed within 30 days of the decision, under a witnessed destruction record signed by two people, and its quantity is reconciled against the batch record. Rejected material is kept in a locked, marked area until it is destroyed, and the area is inspected every 7 days. A decision to reject can be reversed only by the QA Head, with a written justification and additional testing, only if the material has not been destroyed, and only within 7 days of the decision.

## Reprocessing

Reprocessing is allowed only where an approved reprocessing procedure exists for the product and the step, and the reprocessed batch is tested against the full specification. A batch is never reprocessed to bring it back within limits by repeating a test until a passing result is obtained.

## Retain samples

The site retains samples of every released lot. They are stored under the labelled storage condition for 12 months beyond the expiry date of the lot, and they are the responsibility of QA, in a store held at the labelled temperature and checked every day. Samples are examined visually once a year, and any change in appearance is reported as a deviation under SOP-QA-101.

## Records

The disposition of every batch is recorded on the certificate FRM-QA-405 with the name of the person who decided and the time. Certificates and the lists of designates are retained as set in POL-0004. A complaint that concerns a released batch reopens its disposition file under SOP-QA-146. A designate's signature is valid for 12 months from the date of the QA Head's letter and lapses automatically if it is not renewed.
