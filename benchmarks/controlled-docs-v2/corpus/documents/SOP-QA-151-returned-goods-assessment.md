# Returned Goods Assessment

## Scope

Product sometimes comes back to the site after it has been shipped: a customer has ordered too much, a shipment could not be delivered, a batch has been recalled or a distributor has closed. This procedure describes how returned goods are authorised, received, assessed and given a disposition. Product returned because of a complaint is handled as a complaint sample under SOP-QA-146 instead.

## Return authorisation

No return is accepted without a return authorisation. The customer service team requests one on FRM-QA-607, stating the product, the lot, the quantity, the reason and the date of shipment, and QA issues the authorisation with a number in the form RA-nnnn. The authorisation is refused if the goods left the site more than 30 days earlier, and the customer is told so. An authorisation is valid for 14 days, and goods that arrive after that are treated as unauthorised. Packs sent back should weigh no more than 20 kg each so that they can be handled safely. The customer is given the instructions for packing and shipping the goods back, and for temperature-controlled goods, the instructions for the shipper and logger.

## Receipt and quarantine

Returned goods are received at the shipping dock RM-146 (Grade D) and are checked against the authorisation before they are accepted. A return with no authorisation number, or with a number that does not match the goods, is put aside and the sender is contacted within 1 day. The receipt is entered in the returns log within 1 day of arrival. A returned shipper is held for 3 days for inspection before it is discarded, and frozen returns are held at minus 20 degrees C. Accepted returns are placed in quarantine in the returns bay on the same day, with a red label showing the authorisation number, the date and the lot. Refrigerated goods are placed on the quarantine shelf of CR-1 at 2 to 8 degrees C, and other goods stay in the returns bay at 15 to 25 degrees C. Nothing from the returns bay is returned to stock, picked or sold until QA has assessed it. Refrigerated goods should reach the site within 48 hours of leaving the customer, and may wait no more than 5 days for assessment, after which they are rejected unless QA extends the time on the record.

## Temperature-controlled returns

Refrigerated and frozen goods must arrive with an active data logger, and their trace is downloaded on receipt. A return whose trace shows a total excursion within the allowance in SOP-WH-088 for that product type may be considered further; a return outside the allowance, or one that arrived without a working logger, is rejected. The trace is attached to the assessment record, and the shipper and the packing materials are inspected for damage.

## Assessment

A QA reviewer assesses each return on FRM-QA-610. The assessment covers the state of the outer carton and the seals, the identity and lot of the goods, the storage records from the time of shipment to the time of return, the remaining shelf life and any sign of tampering or damage. The reviewer photographs the goods before opening any packaging, and the retention of the photographs follows the product retention plan PRP-0007. The visual examination that was once carried out under SOP-QA-058 has been replaced by this assessment.

## Conditions for restocking

Goods may be restocked only if all of the following are true.

- The seals and packaging are intact and show no sign of opening.
- The storage and transport records are complete and show no unexplained gap.
- Any temperature-controlled goods were within their allowance throughout.
- The remaining shelf life meets the requirement of the policy POL-0012.
- QA has approved the restocking in writing.

If any condition is not met, the goods are rejected. Goods are never restocked on the strength of a verbal assurance from the customer, and QA does not approve restocking of a lot that is the subject of a complaint investigation.

## Decision timing

QA decides on each return within 10 working days of its receipt, and the decision is entered on FRM-QA-610. A return that has not been decided within that time is reported to the QA Head with the reason. While the decision is pending, the goods stay in the returns bay and are not moved.

## Rejected returns

A rejected return is destroyed within 20 working days of the decision. Destruction is witnessed by 2 people, and the certificate lists the authorisation number, the lot and the quantity. The quantity destroyed is reconciled with the quantity received. Where the customer asks for the goods to be returned to them, the request is refused, since the site cannot vouch for the condition of the goods.

## Refunds and credit

The commercial arrangements for refunds or credit to the customer are handled by the customer service team under its own terms, and they do not depend on the quality decision except that a credit for goods that are rejected is not conditional on their destruction being completed.

## Monthly review

Every month, the customer quality officer reviews the returns log: the number of returns by product and reason, the proportion restocked and rejected, the time taken to decide, and any customer with repeated returns. The customer service team also reviews open authorisations every 7 days and cancels any that have lapsed. The results go to the QA Head, and a rising number of returns for one product is examined as a possible sign of a quality or supply problem. A customer with more than 3 returns for the same reason in 12 months is contacted by the customer quality officer to find the cause, and the outcome is recorded.
