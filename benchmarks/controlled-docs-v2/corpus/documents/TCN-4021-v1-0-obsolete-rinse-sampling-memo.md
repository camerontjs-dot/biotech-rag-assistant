# Rinse Sampling Memo

## Purpose of the memo

Manufacturing Operations adopted rinse water sampling as the way of checking that product-contact parts were clean at the end of a wash. This memo describes how the rinse sample is taken from a part, what is measured and what result is accepted. It applies to the parts of the filling line and to the washing room where they are cleaned.

## Taking the rinse sample

After the final rinse of a part, the operator collects the last 500 mL of rinse water from the outlet of the washer or from the rinse bath of a manually washed part into a clean glass bottle. Before collection, the outlet is flushed for 1 minute so that the water represents the part and not the pipework. The bottle is rinsed 3 times with the sample water before it is filled, closed and labelled with the part name, the wash date and the time.

## What is measured

The sample is tested for total organic carbon in the laboratory. The result is compared with the limit for the product that was made on the part, and the limit is written on the cleaning record before the sample is taken. Conductivity is read at the same time, and a conductivity above the final rinse limit stops the release of the part regardless of the carbon result.

## Acceptance

A rinse sample is accepted when the total organic carbon result is 5 ppm or lower. A result between 5 and 7 ppm is repeated once with a fresh rinse of the part. A second result over 5 ppm requires the part to be washed again from the start of the sequence. The result is entered on the rinse result sheet by the laboratory analyst, and the production supervisor signs the cleaning record after seeing it.

## Timing

The sample is taken within 30 minutes of the end of the final rinse and reaches the laboratory within 2 hours. Parts wait in a covered rack until the result is known, and a part whose result is more than 24 hours late is washed again.

## Points to watch

Rinse sampling reflects only the residue that is carried away by the water. Product that is trapped in a dead leg, a blind thread or a crevice may not appear in the rinse, and a part with such features is given extra attention during the visual inspection. Detergent left in the part will usually appear in the conductivity, and the analyst reports any unusual difference between the carbon and the conductivity.

## Records

The rinse result sheet is kept with the cleaning record for the batch that followed the wash, and it shows the part, the wash date, the time of sampling, the carbon result in ppm, the conductivity in uS/cm and the analyst's initials. Sheets are reviewed by the production supervisor within 1 day of the result and by QA when the batch record is reviewed. A result that was repeated shows both values on the same line.
