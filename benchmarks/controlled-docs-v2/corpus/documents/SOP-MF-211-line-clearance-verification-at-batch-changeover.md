# Line Clearance Verification at Batch Changeover

## Why the check exists

Most mix-ups in packaging and filling come from something small that was left behind: a label from the last batch, a handful of stoppers from another lot, a page of the previous batch record. Line clearance is the deliberate check that the line and its surroundings hold only what belongs to the next batch. It is done at every changeover, after cleaning and before the first component of the new batch enters the line. Cleaning itself is covered by SOP-MF-207.

## Who takes part

The check is done by two people together: the line operator who will run the batch and the production supervisor. A QA line clearance inspector then verifies the result. The operator and the supervisor cannot be the same person, and neither may be someone who worked on the previous batch on that line without a break. All three sign FRM-MF-311.

## When the check is required

- At every changeover between products or lots on FL-2, whether Type A or Type B in the terms of SOP-MF-207.
- After a line stop of more than 8 hours during which the line was left open or unattended.
- After any repair or maintenance that involved removing components from the line.
- At the start of a campaign after a full clean.

## The twelve points

FRM-MF-311 lists 12 points. The operator and the supervisor check each one physically, not from memory and not from the previous checklist.

| Point | What is checked |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Documents of the previous batch have been removed from the room |
| 2 | Vials, stoppers and seals of the previous batch have been removed |
| 3 | Printed labels and leaflets of the previous batch have been removed and reconciled |
| 4 | Equipment status tags read clean and match the cleaning record |
| 5 | Change parts fitted are the ones named for the next product |
| 6 | A new sterile tubing set is fitted and its lot is recorded |
| 7 | Machine recipe and settings have been changed to the next product |
| 8 | No stray components, tools or materials are on or under the line |
| 9 | Waste bins are empty and waste has left the room |
| 10 | Filters, gaskets and needle seats have been inspected |
| 11 | Barrier gloves are within their change interval and intact |
| 12 | The environmental sampling plan for the next session has been confirmed |

A point that cannot be confirmed is marked as failed and the check stops at that point. It is put right, and the whole list is then checked again from point 1.

## Label reconciliation

The quantity of labels issued for the previous batch must equal the quantity used, plus the quantity returned unused, plus the quantity destroyed. Two people count printed rolls and leaflets independently, and the variance must be zero. A difference of even one label is investigated before the line is cleared, because a lost label is a label that could be on the wrong product. The count is written on the labelling reconciliation sheet and attached to the batch record.

## Stray components

No stray component of any kind is accepted on the line. A single vial, stopper or seal that is found is reported, put with the previous batch's material, and its lot is recorded. If the material found does not belong to the previous batch either, the finding is escalated to the production supervisor at once and the clearance does not continue until its origin is known.

## Verification by QA

When the operator and the supervisor have completed the twelve points, they request verification by the QA line clearance inspector, who attends within 30 minutes of the request. The inspector repeats a sample of the checks, always including points 2, 3 and 8, and looks at the line from the operator's position and from the opposite side. The inspector signs FRM-MF-311 only after all points pass. If the inspector fails any point, the clearance is repeated in full and the failure is recorded on the form.

## Validity of the clearance

A clearance is valid for 8 hours from the inspector's signature, provided the line stays idle and closed. If the line is not started within 8 hours, or if the room is used for anything else in that time, the clearance lapses and is done again in full. A clearance is never carried over from one shift to another without a fresh signature, and the time of signature is written next to it.

## Type B changeovers

For a Type B changeover the clearance also depends on the cleaning verification in SOP-MF-207. The inspector confirms that the swab results are available, or, where the rule for the product allows it, that filling will not start until they are. The swab result sheet FRM-MF-305, prepared from the swab kit MAT-3120 used under SOP-MF-207, is attached to FRM-MF-311.

## Records

The completed FRM-MF-311, the labelling reconciliation and the cleaning record are attached to the batch record. The reviewer under SOP-QA-133 checks that the clearance time precedes the first fill time and that the signatures are complete. A missing clearance record is a deviation under SOP-QA-101.
