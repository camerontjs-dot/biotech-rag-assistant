# Loop Sanitization and Return to Service

## When this procedure applies

Water that stands still in a pipe grows a film of organisms, and a loop that has been opened, stopped or found out of limits has to be cleaned before its water can be used again. This procedure describes the routine sanitization of the purified water loop LP-01 and the water for injection loop LP-02, the sanitization that follows a stoppage or a repair, and the sampling that has to be passed before a loop returns to service. Routine monitoring is in SOP-EN-152, and the limits are in SPC-2252.

## Planned sanitization

Both loops are sanitised with hot water on a fixed schedule.

| Loop | Method | Conditions | Frequency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LP-02, WFI | Hot water | At least 80 degrees C at the coldest point for at least 90 minutes | Monthly |
| LP-01, PW | Hot water | 80 degrees C for 2 hours | Quarterly |

The temperature is read on the return probe EQ-1210 for LP-02 and confirmed at the coldest point on each loop, which is the return point PT-28 on LP-02 and PT-12 on LP-01 after the loop has been fully heated. The time starts when that point reaches the target temperature, not when the heaters are switched on. The utilities technician records the temperature at the coldest point every 10 minutes during the hold on FRM-EN-305.

## Notice to production

Production is told at least 48 hours before a planned sanitization, because the loop is out of use for the whole cycle and for the sampling that follows. The utilities engineer agrees the date with the production planner and posts it on the utilities board. A sanitization is moved only with the agreement of both parties, and it is never cancelled simply because production would prefer to use the water.

## Steps of a sanitization

1. Confirm that no product batch or cleaning activity is drawing water from the loop.
2. Bring the loop to temperature with the tank and the distribution pumps running, and record the start on FRM-EN-305.
3. Read the coldest point every 10 minutes and note any point that lags behind the others.
4. Hold the required temperature for the required time.
5. Allow the loop to cool while circulating.
6. Flush the loop with 3 loop volumes of water, running to drain, until the in-line conductivity reads 1.0 uS/cm or lower.
7. Record the end of the cycle and the flush result, and pass the loop to sampling.

## Return to service

After a sanitization, a loop returns to service only when 2 consecutive daily samples at every point meet the alert levels in SPC-2252 and the in-line TOC reading is below its alert level. Sampling begins on the day after the flush. The utilities engineer and a QC representative sign FRM-EN-305 to release the loop, and QA countersigns when the sanitization followed a stoppage or a repair rather than the planned schedule. Until the release is signed, water from the loop is not used for product, cleaning or equipment rinsing.

## Stoppage of a loop

A loop that has been stopped for more than 4 hours is sanitised before it returns to service, whatever the cause. A loop that has been stopped for 4 hours or less is restarted, allowed to circulate at temperature for 30 minutes, and sampled at its return point before use; a result at the alert level or above means the full cycle is carried out. Any stoppage is entered on the round sheet with its start and end times, so that the utilities engineer can see how long each stop lasted.

## Repairs and modifications

A repair that opens the pipework needs a work permit and is followed by a sanitization of the affected loop before the pipe is returned to use. New pipework is assessed for dead legs; a branch that is longer than six pipe diameters beyond the main line is reported to the utilities engineer before it is put into use. A modification to the loop, its instruments or its layout goes through SOP-QA-118 before work begins.

## Tank inspection

The WFI tank TK-201 is drained and inspected once a year, and the PW tank TK-101 is inspected on the same visit. The inspector checks the internal surfaces, the spray ball, the vent filter housing and the level instrument. The inspection is combined with a sanitization of the loop and tank so that the system is cleaned once, not twice, and the result of the annual sanitization is entered in the record for the loop.

## Safety

Hot water at 80 degrees C can cause serious burns. Technicians wear insulated gloves and face protection when working near open valves, and warning signs are posted at the entrances to RM-120 and RM-122 during a hot cycle. Nobody works alone on a loop while it is hot, and a burn is reported to the site nurse and entered in the safety log.

## Records

The completed FRM-EN-305, the temperature trace, the flush result and the release signatures are filed with the loop record and kept under POL-0004.
